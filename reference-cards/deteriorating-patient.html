🚩 Between the Flags

🫑 RR is the first sign to change in deterioration!

NSW Health's clinical deterioration detection system. Vital sign observations are charted against coloured zones on a standardised observation chart.

ZoneMeaningAction
Yellow ZoneObservation outside normal rangeIncrease monitoring, notify nurse in charge
Red Zone (Between the Flags)Observation in critical rangeImmediate escalation: call MET/RRT using ISBAR
A single observation in the red zone = urgent escalation. Don't wait for a second reading or for the patient to "look" sick.

🅰️ ABCDE Assessment

💡 Don't wait for the crash, escalate early.
A B C D E
Airway • Breathing • Circulation • Disability • Exposure

Systematic approach: fix A before assessing B, fix B before C, etc.

A — Airway

  • Is the airway patent? Can the patient speak?
  • Look, listen, feel for obstruction
  • Suction, head tilt/chin lift, jaw thrust if needed
  • Consider adjuncts (OPA, NPA) if trained

B — Breathing

  • Rate, depth, pattern, effort
  • SpO2: target 94-98% (88-92% in COPD)
  • Auscultate: bilateral air entry, adventitious sounds
  • Apply O2 as prescribed/per emergency protocol

C — Circulation

  • HR, BP, capillary refill, skin colour/temp
  • Check for bleeding (visible and concealed)
  • IV access: establish if not already in situ
  • Fluid resuscitation as prescribed

D — Disability

  • GCS or AVPU (Alert/Voice/Pain/Unresponsive)
  • Pupil size and reactivity (PERRLA)
  • BGL: check and treat hypo/hyperglycaemia
  • Limb movement and sensation

E — Exposure

  • Full body check: rashes, wounds, oedema, bleeding
  • Temperature: fever or hypothermia
  • Maintain dignity: cover patient appropriately
  • Review all available information: notes, charts, medications

📞 Escalation

When to Call for Help

  • Any red-zone observation on the obs chart
  • New onset confusion or reduced consciousness
  • Acute change in HR, BP, RR, SpO2
  • Significant bleeding
  • Chest pain, acute SOB
  • Seizure
  • You are worried about your patient (clinical intuition counts)
"I'm worried about my patient" is a valid reason to escalate. You do not need to wait for a measurable trigger.

How to Escalate: ISBAR

  • Identify: yourself, patient, location
  • Situation: what's happening right now
  • Background: relevant history, current treatment
  • Assessment: your ABCDE findings, what's abnormal
  • Recommendation: what you need (review, intervention, MET call)

✅ Documentation

  • Time of deterioration recognition
  • ABCDE findings and actions taken
  • Who was notified and when (name, role, time)
  • Response to interventions
  • Ongoing monitoring plan
Document in real time. If you called the MET team at 14:32, write 14:32, not "this afternoon."
