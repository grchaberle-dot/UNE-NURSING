\uD83D\uDD0D The Assessment Sequence

For most body systems, work in this order:

1. Inspect2. Palpate3. Percuss4. Auscultate

The abdomen is the exception. Auscultate BEFORE percussing or palpating:

1. Inspect2. Auscultate3. Percuss4. Palpate
Palpating the abdomen first would alter the bowel sounds you are about to listen for. This order question comes up constantly in exams.

\uD83E\uDDE0 Neurological

Level of Consciousness

Alert and oriented to: person, place, time, situation. Documented as A&O x4 if oriented to all four.

Orientation Descriptors

Awake, alert, restless, drowsy, sedated, confused.

PERRLA

Pupils Equal, Round, Reactive to Light, and Accommodation.

Neck

  • Use of accessory muscles

\u2764\uFE0F Chest: Heart Sounds

Auscultate with both the diaphragm and the bell.

PointLocation
AorticRight 2nd intercostal space
PulmonicLeft 2nd intercostal space
Erb's PointLeft 3rd intercostal space (S1, S2)
TricuspidLower left sternal border, 4th intercostal space
MitralLeft 5th intercostal space, medial to the midclavicular line

Sounds: normal, murmurs, muffled, gallop, bruits, whooshing. Rhythm: regular or irregular.

A-P-E-T-M is the order down the chest: Aortic, Pulmonic, Erb's, Tricuspid, Mitral. "All People Enjoy Time Magazine."

\uD83C\uDF2C\uFE0F Chest: Breath Sounds

Auscultate posteriorly and laterally, comparing side to side.

  • Sounds: clear, wheezing, crackles, diminished
  • Rhythm: regular or irregular
Listening only to the front of the chest misses the lung bases, which is exactly where fluid collects in heart failure.

\uD83E\uDDA0 Abdomen

Inspection

Symmetrical, rounded, distended, ascites, scars.

Bowel Sounds (all four quadrants)

Assess RLQ, RUQ, LUQ and LLQ. Document each as: absent, hypoactive, normoactive, or hyperactive.

Remember the altered order for the abdomen: inspect, auscultate, percuss, then palpate.

\uD83D\uDCAA Extremities & Perfusion

Upper

  • Grip strength
  • Range of motion
  • Radial pulse
  • Capillary refill

Lower

  • Strength and range of motion
  • Dorsalis pedis pulse
  • Posterior tibial pulse
  • Capillary refill
  • Oedema: location, pitting or non-pitting

Pulse Grading

GradeMeaning
0Pulse is absent
1+Diminished
2+Normal
3+Full
4+Bounding, strong

Capillary refill time: normal is under 2 to 3 seconds.

Pitting oedema is graded 0, +1, +2, +3, +4.

\uD83D\uDCCF Pain Assessment

  • Intensity: 0 (no pain) to 10 (worst pain)
  • Location
  • Description: the patient's own words
Pain is subjective. Document what the patient says, not what you think they look like.

\uD83D\uDDFA\uFE0F Concept Mapping

A concept map links the patient's problems back to their history and forward to goals. Structure:

  • Centre: patient info and medical history
  • Each branch: one nursing diagnosis
  • Under each diagnosis: supporting data (the cues that justify it)
  • Then: goals for that diagnosis
  • Arrows between branches: show how the problems influence each other
The arrows are the point. Anyone can list four problems. A concept map shows you understand that reduced mobility worsens skin integrity, which raises infection risk.

\uD83D\uDCCB Structuring a Patient Report

What to capture before a shift, grouped the way handover expects it:

  • Identifiers: room, age, admit date, diagnosis, treating team
  • Alerts: code status (full, NFR, limited), allergies
  • Safety: falls, restraints, aspiration, absconding risk
  • Isolation: contact, droplet, airborne, neutropenic
  • Activity: independent, bedrest, assist level
  • Diet: regular, NPO, diabetic, fluid restriction, cardiac, renal
  • By system: cardiac, respiratory, GI/GU, neuro, musculoskeletal, skin
  • Lines and fluids: IV gauge, site, insertion and change dates, infusions, antibiotics
  • Pain: location, last analgesia, next dose due
  • Labs: FBC, electrolytes, renal function, coagulation
Pair this with CHIMNEOPS for your written notes and ISBAR for escalation. Collect by system, document by CHIMNEOPS, escalate by ISBAR.
Assessment findings and normal ranges vary by facility and patient population. Always follow your UNE unit guide and your facility's assessment documentation.
