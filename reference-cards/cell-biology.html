\uD83D\uDD2C Prokaryotic vs Eukaryotic Cells

FeatureProkaryoticEukaryotic
General organisationSimpler structureMore complex structure
NucleusNo membrane-bound nucleusContains a membrane-bound nucleus
Genetic materialNot enclosed by a nuclear membraneLocated within the nucleus
Membrane-bound organellesNot part of the basic structurePresent, e.g. mitochondria and endoplasmic reticulum

Four main prokaryotic structures

  • Cell membrane: controls what enters and leaves the cell
  • Cytoplasm: internal fluid region where cellular processes occur
  • Ribosomes: structures involved in protein production
  • Genetic material: contains hereditary information but is not enclosed in a nucleus
Bacteria are prokaryotic. Human cells are eukaryotic. That difference is exactly why some antibiotics can target bacteria without destroying our own cells.

\uD83E\uDDF1 The Cell Membrane: Fluid Mosaic Model

The cell membrane is a selectively permeable boundary. The fluid mosaic model describes a flexible phospholipid membrane containing embedded proteins and other components.

  • Fluid: membrane components can move within the membrane
  • Mosaic: different proteins and molecules are distributed throughout the phospholipid structure
  • Selective permeability: some substances cross easily, others require membrane proteins or are restricted

\uD83D\uDD17 Cell Junctions

JunctionFunctionWhere Found
Tight junctionsSeal cells together, prevent leakageBladder lining, intestinal epithelium
Adherens junctionsLink cells via actin filamentsEpithelial tissues
DesmosomesAnchor cells together, resist tearingSkin, heart muscle
Gap junctionsAllow direct cell-to-cell communicationCardiac muscle, smooth muscle, neurons
Gap junctions in cardiac muscle are why the heart beats as a coordinated unit: electrical signals pass directly between cells through these channels.

\uD83E\uDDEB Membrane Transport

Passive (no energy required)

  • Diffusion: movement of solute from high to low concentration
  • Osmosis: movement of water across a semi-permeable membrane toward higher solute concentration
  • Facilitated diffusion: uses a membrane protein but no energy

Active (energy required)

  • Active transport: moves substances against the concentration gradient using energy
  • Allows cells to maintain concentration differences that passive transport alone could not

Tonicity

SolutionEffect on the Cell
IsotonicNo net water movement, the cell stays the same
HypotonicWater moves INTO the cell, the cell swells and may burst
HypertonicWater moves OUT of the cell, the cell shrinks
Tonicity is why IV fluid choice matters clinically. The wrong fluid shifts water into or out of cells with real consequences.

\u2696\uFE0F Homeostasis

Homeostasis is the maintenance of a relatively stable internal environment despite external change.

Negative feedback

The response opposes the original change, returning the variable toward its set point. This is the dominant mechanism in the body.

Example: temperature regulation. Rising temperature triggers sweating and vasodilation, which cool the body back toward the set point.

Positive feedback

The response amplifies the original change, driving the process to completion. Much rarer in the body.

Almost every homeostatic mechanism you will meet is negative feedback. If a question describes a process being amplified rather than corrected, it is positive feedback.

\uD83E\uDDEC Cell Division

  • Mitosis: produces two genetically identical daughter cells. Used for growth and tissue repair.
  • Meiosis: produces gametes with half the chromosome number. Used for sexual reproduction.

\uD83E\uDDF0 Tissue Repair

  • Regeneration: replacement with the same type of cells. The ideal outcome.
  • Fibrosis: replacement with scar tissue (collagen). Results in functional loss.
  • High regeneration capacity: epithelial tissue, bone, blood
  • Low regeneration capacity: cardiac muscle, nervous tissue
This is why heart attacks cause permanent damage. Cardiac muscle cannot regenerate, so it scars, and scar tissue does not contract.

Use directional terms in documentation instead of vague words. "Wound to the distal anterior left lower leg" tells the next nurse exactly where to look.
