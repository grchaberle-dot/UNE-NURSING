🔍 Pressure Injury Stages

Pressure injury stages 1 through 4: progressive depth of tissue damage

🩹 Pressure Injury Staging

⚠️ Stage 1: non-blanchable redness is the early warning.

Stage 1

Intact skin with non-blanchable redness. May be painful, firm, soft, warmer or cooler than surrounding tissue.

Stage 2

Partial-thickness skin loss. Shallow open wound with red/pink wound bed, or intact/ruptured blister. No slough.

Stage 3

Full-thickness skin loss. Subcutaneous fat may be visible. Bone, tendon, muscle NOT exposed. Slough may be present but does not obscure depth.

Stage 4

Full-thickness tissue loss with exposed bone, tendon, or muscle. Slough or eschar may be present. Often includes undermining and tunnelling.

Unstageable

Full-thickness tissue loss where the base is covered by slough or eschar. True depth cannot be determined until debrided.

⭐ Turn every 2 hours on placement. Prevention > treatment.

🛡️ Pressure Injury Prevention

📊 Braden Scale: lower score = higher risk.

Key Interventions

  • Reposition every 2 hours (minimum)
  • Use pressure-relieving mattress and cushions
  • Maintain adequate nutrition and hydration
  • Keep skin clean and dry
  • Inspect pressure points every shift
  • Use Braden Scale for risk assessment
  • Float heels off the bed (pillow under calves)
  • Avoid dragging or shearing during repositioning
Prevention is always better than treatment. A pressure injury that never develops costs nothing. One that does can take months to heal and cause significant suffering.

🩹 Wound Assessment Documentation

  • Location: anatomical site
  • Size: length × width × depth (in cm)
  • Wound bed: colour (red=granulation, yellow=slough, black=necrotic)
  • Exudate: amount, colour, consistency, odour
  • Edges: undermining, tunnelling, rolled, flat
  • Surrounding skin: erythema, maceration, induration
  • Pain: at rest, during dressing changes
  • Signs of infection: increasing pain, redness, warmth, swelling, purulent exudate, odour, fever
