\u2728 The Six Rights of Drug Administration

1. Right Medication

Compare the label to the medication order form 3 times:

  1. Before removing from the drawer/shelf
  2. As the amount ordered is removed
  3. Before returning the container to storage

2. Right Dose

When performing calculations or conversions, have another qualified nurse check the calculated dose.

3. Right Client

Check the medication form against the client's identification bracelet AND ask the client to state their name.

4. Right Route

If the order doesn't specify a route, consult the prescriber. If the route is not recommended, alert the prescriber immediately.

5. Right Time

Know why medication is ordered at specific times. Some must act at certain times (e.g. insulin before meals).

6. Right Documentation

Document: client name, medication name, time, dose, route, frequency. Sign immediately after administration.

\uD83E\uDDEA Pharmacokinetics (ADME)

What the body does to a drug

  • Absorption: Drug enters systemic circulation after administration
  • Distribution: Drug moves from vascular space to tissues
  • Metabolism: Irreversible transformation of parent compound into metabolites (mainly liver)
  • Elimination: Removal of substances from the body (mainly kidneys)
ADME = Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, Elimination. The drug's journey: in, around, changed, out.

\uD83D\uDC89 Administration Routes

Oral Routes

  • Sublingual: Under tongue. Rapid absorption into bloodstream. Don't swallow.
  • Buccal: Against cheek mucosa. Bypasses liver for greater potency.
  • Oral (PO): Solid (tablet, capsule, pill) or liquid (syrup, suspension, emulsion, elixir)
Elixir = alcohol-based. Wait 30 min before giving water for maximum absorption. Suspension = shake before use.

Topical Routes

  • Dermatologic: Lotions, liniment, ointments, powder
  • Ophthalmic: Eye instillation and irrigation
  • Otic: Ear instillation (remove cerumen, pus, foreign body)
  • Nasal: Decongestants, steroids, calcitonin
  • Inhaled: Nebuliser, metered-dose inhaler
  • Vaginal: Tablet, douche, jelly, foam, suppository

\uD83D\uDCCB Components of a Complete Order

  • Client name (last and first)
  • Medication name
  • Strength of medication (if required)
  • Dosage to be administered
  • Route of administration
  • Specific directions, including frequency
  • Reason for administration (if PRN)
  • Prescriber's signature
