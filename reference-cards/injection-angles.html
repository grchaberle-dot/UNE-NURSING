Parenteral Administration: ID, SC, IM, IV

10-15\u00B0

Intradermal (ID)

  • Location: Inner lower arm, upper chest/back, beneath scapula
  • Uses: Allergy testing, tuberculin (TB/Mantoux) testing, vaccinations
  • Gauge: 25, 26, or 27
  • Needle length: 3/8", 5/8", or 1/2"
  • Technique: Bevel UP. Inject slowly (3-5 seconds) to form a wheal/bleb
  • Key rule: Do NOT massage the site (prevents irritation, prevents absorption into subcutaneous layer)
Reaches: Under the epidermis only (between epidermis and dermis)
45\u00B0

Subcutaneous (SC)

  • Uses: Vaccines, heparin, preoperative medication, insulin, narcotics
  • Key rules: Only small doses. Rotate injection sites to minimise tissue damage
  • Gauge/length: Same as intradermal
Reaches: Subcutaneous tissue (fatty layer below dermis)
90\u00B0

Intramuscular (IM)

  • Needle length: 1" to 1.5" (to reach muscle layer)
  • Technique: Clean site with alcohol swab. Inject slowly to allow tissue to accommodate volume
  • Common sites: Deltoid, vastus lateralis, ventrogluteal, dorsogluteal
Reaches: Muscle layer (below subcutaneous tissue)

Intravenous (IV)

  • Methods: Mixed in large volumes of IV fluid, or bolus through existing IV line/heparin lock
  • Key point: Fastest route of administration, immediate onset
  • Risk: Cannot be retrieved once administered, higher risk of adverse reactions
Reaches: Directly into the vein (bloodstream)
The angle progression: ID is nearly flat (10-15\u00B0), SC is halfway (45\u00B0), IM is straight down (90\u00B0). Each goes deeper: epidermis \u2192 subcutaneous fat \u2192 muscle. The deeper you go, the steeper the angle.
