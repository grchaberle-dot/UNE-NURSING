\uD83D\uDDE3\uFE0F Therapeutic Communication Techniques

  • Simple language: Replace complex terminology with words the patient understands
  • Appropriate pace: Speak slowly enough for the patient to process information
  • Open questions: Invite the patient to describe concerns, symptoms and preferences
  • Clarification: Ask follow-up questions when the patient's response is uncertain
  • Paraphrasing: Restate the patient's concern to confirm understanding
  • Summarising: Review main information and agreed next steps
  • Silence: Allow time for thought, emotional responses and questions
  • Reassurance: Acknowledge distress without dismissing the patient's concern
  • Teach-back: Ask the patient to explain important instructions in their own words
Teach-back is the gold standard for checking understanding. "Can you tell me in your own words what we just discussed?" If they can't explain it back, you need to re-teach.

\uD83E\uDDE0 Clinical Reasoning: Hypoglycaemia Example

Assessment Before Intervention

  1. Checked patient's BGL before administering insulin
  2. Recognised reading was below expected range
  3. Reported to supervising RN
  4. Observed for hypoglycaemia signs: sweating, unresponsiveness, slurred speech, tremor, unsteadiness
  5. Offered breakfast and allowed time to settle
  6. Assisted RN to contact doctor
  7. Obtained consent before rechecking BGL
  8. Confirmed later reading was within safe range for prescribed insulin
  9. Administered charted insulin SC under RN supervision
  10. Continued monitoring at short intervals
NEVER administer medication without first checking relevant assessment findings and prescription. Escalate abnormal observations or uncertainty to an RN or medical practitioner.

\uD83D\uDC8A Medication Safety Principles

  • Use only approved routes of administration
  • Confirm medication, formulation and route are appropriate for the patient
  • Check prescription and relevant patient information before administration
  • Consider allergies, medical history, current observations and contraindications
  • Clarify any unusual, incomplete or unsafe order before proceeding
  • Do not alter or administer medication in a way that is outside policy
  • Use appropriate supervision when required
  • Document medication, route, response and any concerns accurately
  • Communicate medication-related risks during handover
  • Report errors, near misses and adverse events promptly
Real case: A nurse administered oral tablets through a patient's IV cannula. Patient deteriorated and died. Criminal negligence and manslaughter proceedings followed. Route of administration is CRITICAL.

\uD83D\uDD04 Rolfe's Reflective Model

What?

What happened? What actions or decisions occurred? Describe the event objectively.

So What?

Why was this significant? What were the consequences or learning points? What knowledge, policy or standard applied?

Now What?

What should be done differently in future practice? What specific change will you make?

Reflection is NOT just describing what happened. It requires analysis of actions, patient responses, professional responsibilities and possible alternatives. It must lead to a realistic practice improvement.

\u2696\uFE0F Professional Accountability

  • Practise safely and competently within scope
  • Maintain appropriate knowledge and skills
  • Seek supervision and assistance when required
  • Protect patient rights, dignity, privacy and confidentiality
  • Document care accurately
  • Report unsafe, unethical or unlawful practice
  • Participate in continuing professional development
  • Adhere to NMBA RN Standards for Practice, ICN Code of Ethics, Code of Conduct

Privacy & Confidentiality in Practice

  • Share patient info only with people who have a legitimate care role
  • Protect written and electronic records
  • Dispose of printed info securely (locked confidential waste bins)
  • Don't discuss patients in public or inappropriate settings
  • Discard printed handover sheets immediately after shift

\uD83C\uDFE5 Person-Centred Care: Older Adults

Erikson's Integrity vs Despair (Stage 8: Late Adulthood)

People reflect on their lives and may experience meaning and satisfaction, or regret and despair. Supporting older patients means recognising both strengths and distress.

Caregiver Support

  • Listen to concerns without judgement
  • Assess caregiver wellbeing and burnout risk
  • Advocate for culturally and linguistically appropriate services
  • Discuss respite care, home care and community resources
  • Support communication planning as abilities change
  • Encourage maintaining physical, emotional and social wellbeing
  • Include both patient and carer in decisions where possible
Don't interpret a caregiver's difficulty as lack of commitment. They may be balancing love, responsibility, cultural expectations, financial limitations and concern for personal independence.

\u2705 The 12-Step Professional Practice Framework

  1. Prepare: Review patient situation, risks, communication needs, cultural preferences
  2. Introduce: State your name, role, purpose. Confirm patient identity.
  3. Establish rapport: Respectful language, appropriate body language, active listening, calm tone
  4. Assess: Gather information, observe verbal and nonverbal cues, identify risks
  5. Explain: Describe procedures, options, benefits, risks in appropriate language
  6. Check understanding: Teach-back, written info, visual aids
  7. Obtain participation and consent: Invite questions, respect preferences, involve patient in decisions
  8. Act safely: Follow scope of practice, medication standards, infection control, policies
  9. Collaborate: Involve MDT, family, support people (with respect for privacy)
  10. Handover: Communicate relevant information using ISBAR
  11. Document: Record assessments, interventions, responses, education, consent, follow-up
  12. Reflect and improve: Seek feedback, analyse outcomes, identify changes for future practice
