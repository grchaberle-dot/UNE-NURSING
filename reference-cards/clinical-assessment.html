Glasgow Coma Scale assessment: eye, verbal and motor responses

🧠 Neurological Assessment

👁️ PERRLA: check every neuro patient.

GCS (Glasgow Coma Scale)

ResponseBestWorst
Eye Opening4 (spontaneous)1 (none)
Verbal5 (oriented)1 (none)
Motor6 (obeys commands)1 (none)
GCS ranges 3-15. Normal = 15. GCS ≤ 8 = cannot protect own airway, consider intubation.
E4 V5 M6 = 15
Eye 4 + Verbal 5 + Motor 6 = normal GCS of 15.

Key Neuro Assessments

  • PERRLA: Pupils Equal, Round, Reactive to Light and Accommodation
  • Romberg test: Balance assessment (feet together, eyes closed)
  • Ataxia: Uncoordinated movement (cerebellar)
  • CN III, IV, VI control eye movement: test with H pattern

🦴 Musculoskeletal

🦴 Babinski: normal in babies, abnormal in adults.

Muscle Strength Scale (0-5)

ScoreResponse
0No movement
1Trace movement
2Movement with gravity eliminated
3Movement against gravity
4Movement against some resistance
5Normal strength
  • Always compare bilaterally
  • Check for scoliosis: unequal shoulders, uneven waist

👶 Paediatric Assessment

  • Children: smaller airways, larger tongue, higher metabolic rate
  • Gross motor: head control → rolling → sitting → crawling → walking
  • SIDS prevention: infants sleep on their backs
  • Babinski: toes fan outward when sole stroked. Normal in infants, abnormal in adults.
  • Ear drops (<3 years): pull ear down and back
  • Acrocyanosis (hands/feet) may be normal in newborns. Central cyanosis (lips/tongue) always abnormal.

🤰 Pregnancy

  • Prenatal nutrients: folic acid, iron, calcium, DHA
  • True labour: regular contractions, increasing intensity, cervical dilation
  • False labour: irregular, no cervical change
  • Flank pain + fever: may indicate pyelonephritis

👴 Ageing

  • Older adults may have lower baseline temp. Even modest fever = serious.
  • Skin turgor: assess on forehead or sternum, not hand.
  • Osteoporosis: decreased bone density, increased fracture risk.

🌬️ Respiratory

  • Atelectasis: incentive spirometry, deep breathing, early mobilisation
  • Percussion: resonance (normal), dullness (fluid/mass), hyperresonance (pneumothorax)
  • Chronic hypoxia: cyanosis, digital clubbing, polycythaemia

❤️ Cardiovascular

  • PAD: intermittent claudication = calf pain during walking, relieved by rest
  • Oedema: elevating legs may reduce when heart-related
  • Distal perfusion: capillary refill, skin colour, temp, peripheral pulses
  • Melanoma ABCDE: Asymmetry, Border, Colour, Diameter, Evolving
