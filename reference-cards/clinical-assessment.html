Deep Tendon Reflex Scale

ScoreResponse
0No response
1Diminished response
2Normal response
3Brisk response
4Very brisk/hyperactive with clonus

Key Neuro Assessments

  • GCS: Glasgow Coma Scale (eye opening + verbal + motor response)
  • PERRLA: Pupils Equal, Round, Reactive to Light and Accommodation
  • Romberg test: Stand feet together, arms at sides, close eyes. Assess balance.
  • Ataxia: Lack of voluntary coordination, clumsy, unsteady, uncoordinated movement
  • Cerebellum: Brain region responsible for balance and coordination

Cranial Nerves for Eye Movement

NerveFunction
CN II (Optic)Vision
CN III (Oculomotor)Eye movement and pupil constriction
CN IV (Trochlear)Downward and inward eye movement
CN VI (Abducens)Lateral eye movement
CN III, IV, VI all control eye movement. Test by asking patient to follow your finger in an H pattern.

  • Always compare strength bilaterally
  • Scoliosis: Check for unequal shoulder height, uneven waist, one hip higher

\uD83D\uDC76 Paediatric Assessment

  • Respiratory compromise: Children have smaller airways, larger tongue, higher metabolic rate
  • Gross motor: Head control \u2192 rolling \u2192 sitting \u2192 crawling \u2192 standing \u2192 walking
  • SIDS prevention: Place infants on their backs to sleep
  • Babinski reflex: Toes fan outward when sole stroked. Normal in infants, abnormal in adults.
  • Ear drops (<3 years): Pull ear down and back
  • Acrocyanosis (hands/feet): May be normal in newborns. Central cyanosis (lips/tongue): Always abnormal, needs intervention.
  • Growth charts: Consistent tracking below 5th or above 95th percentile requires assessment

\uD83E\uDD30 Pregnancy Assessment

  • Prenatal nutrients: Folic acid, iron, calcium, DHA
  • True labour: Regular contractions that increase in intensity and duration, produce cervical dilation
  • False labour: Irregular, don't progressively increase, no cervical change
  • Flank pain + fever: May indicate pyelonephritis (kidney infection)
  • Emergencies: Ectopic pregnancy, preeclampsia, placental abruption
  • Breastfeeding: Provides ideal nutrition, antibodies, reduces infection risk

\uD83D\uDC74 Ageing Assessment

  • Fever: Older adults may have lower baseline temperature. Even a modest fever may indicate serious infection.
  • Skin turgor: Decreased with age-related elasticity loss. Assess on forehead or sternum, not hand.
  • Osteoporosis: Decreased bone density with increased fracture risk

\uD83C\uDF2C\uFE0F Respiratory Assessment

  • Atelectasis interventions: Incentive spirometry, deep breathing, early mobilisation
  • Normal percussion: Resonance
  • Dullness: May suggest fluid or mass
  • Hyperresonance: May suggest pneumothorax
  • Chronic hypoxia signs: Cyanosis, digital clubbing, polycythaemia

\u2764\uFE0F Cardiovascular Assessment

  • PAD (Peripheral Arterial Disease): Intermittent claudication = calf pain during walking, relieved by rest
  • Oedema: Elevating legs may reduce when heart-related
  • Arterial insufficiency: Dependent positioning may improve arterial blood flow
  • Distal perfusion: Assess capillary refill, skin colour, temperature, peripheral pulses
  • Melanoma ABCDE: Asymmetry, Border irregularity, Colour variation, Diameter, Evolving appearance

\uD83E\uDDE0 Mental Health Priority

First, always assess safety and risk of harm to self or others. Mental health emergencies: suicidal ideation, homicidal ideation, acute psychosis, severe agitation require immediate safety assessment and intervention.

Inheritance Patterns

PatternPrincipleExamples
Autosomal DominantOne abnormal allele can cause disorderFamilial hypercholesterolaemia, Huntington disease, Marfan syndrome
Autosomal RecessiveTwo disease alleles requiredCystic fibrosis, sickle cell anaemia
X-linked RecessiveAllele on X chromosome, males more affectedDuchenne muscular dystrophy, haemophilia A

Chromosomal Disorders

  • Down Syndrome (Trisomy 21): Congenital heart defects, flat nasal bridge, short stature
  • Klinefelter Syndrome (XXY): Male, infertility, small testes, low testosterone, gynecomastia
  • Turner Syndrome (X0): Female, infertility, short stature, webbed neck
Dominant = one allele is enough. Recessive = need two. X-linked = males affected more because they only have one X. Phenotype = genotype + environment.
