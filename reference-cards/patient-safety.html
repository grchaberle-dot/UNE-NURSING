💊 Medication Safety: The Rights

8 RIGHTS
Right Patient • Right Drug • Right Dose • Right Route • Right Time • Right Documentation • Right Reason • Right Response

Checking Process

  • Check patient ID (minimum 3 identifiers)
  • Check medication against prescription
  • Check allergies and adverse reactions
  • Check expiry date and packaging integrity
  • Calculate dose (show working)
  • Observe patient take medication
  • Document immediately after administration
  • Monitor for expected and adverse effects
Never leave medications unattended at the bedside. If you didn't see them swallow it, you can't document that they took it.

🦴 Falls Prevention

⚠️ Falls = leading cause of harm in Australian hospitals.

Risk Factors

  • Age >65
  • Previous falls history
  • Medications (sedatives, antihypertensives, opioids)
  • Impaired mobility or balance
  • Cognitive impairment (delirium, dementia)
  • Visual impairment
  • Continence issues (rushing to toilet)
  • Environmental hazards

Prevention Strategies

  • Falls risk assessment on admission (e.g. Falls Risk Assessment Tool)
  • Bed in lowest position, brakes on
  • Call bell within reach
  • Non-slip footwear
  • Clear pathways, adequate lighting
  • Toileting schedule
  • Medication review
  • Supervision during mobilisation (as indicated)
  • Yellow falls-risk alert (wristband, bed sign)

📝 Incident Reporting

  • Report ALL incidents AND near-misses through your facility's incident reporting system (e.g. IIMS in NSW)
  • Open disclosure: honest communication with patient/family about adverse events
  • No-blame culture: focus on system improvement, not individual punishment
  • Root cause analysis for serious incidents
  • NSQHS Standard 1: Clinical Governance requires incident management systems
Near-misses are gold for learning. Something that almost went wrong reveals a system weakness that CAN be fixed before someone gets hurt.
