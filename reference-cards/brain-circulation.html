🛡️ Structures That Protect the Brain

💡 CSF = brain's shock absorber.

Cranium

Bony skull encloses and protects from mechanical forces.

Meninges

Three connective-tissue membranes covering brain and spinal cord.

Cerebrospinal Fluid

Fluid cushion that supports and protects neural tissue.

Blood-Brain Barrier

Selective barrier limiting harmful substances from blood into brain.

Outside in: cranium → dura mater → arachnoid mater → subarachnoid space (CSF) → pia mater → BBB.

🧬 The Three Meningeal Layers

🧠 DAP: outside → inside. Dura is tough; pia hugs the brain.

Meninges: Dura, Arachnoid, Pia (outside → inside)

LayerCharacteristics
Dura materOutermost, tough, leathery. Has periosteal and meningeal layers. Dural sinuses form between them.
Arachnoid materMiddle. Subarachnoid space beneath contains cerebral blood vessels and CSF.
Pia materThin inner membrane hugging brain surface. Contains capillaries nourishing neural tissue.
DAP
Dura, Arachnoid, Pia — meningeal layers from outside to inside.

⚠️ Meningeal Spaces & Clinical Significance

  • Subdural space: Between dura and arachnoid. Subdural haemorrhage.
  • Subarachnoid space: Contains CSF and major cerebral vessels. Subarachnoid haemorrhage.
  • Arachnoid villi: Absorb CSF into venous blood.
  • Meningitis: Inflammation of meninges. Bacterial = medical emergency.
Meningitis triad: headache, fever, stiff neck.

💧 Cerebrospinal Fluid

Derived from blood plasma, produced in four ventricles.

Circulation

  1. Produced in ventricles
  2. Flows through ventricles
  3. Circulates in subarachnoid space
  4. Passes through central canal of spinal cord
  5. Returns to blood through arachnoid villi

Functions

  • Liquid cushion against injury
  • Suspends brain, reducing effective weight
  • Diffusion medium for nutrients and waste

🚪 Blood-Brain Barrier

Tight junctions between brain capillary endothelial cells. Among least permeable capillaries in body.

Permeable to

  • O2 and CO2
  • Lipids
  • Alcohol, nicotine
  • Anaesthetics
  • Glucose

Impermeable to

  • Metabolic wastes
  • Proteins, toxins
  • K+ ions
  • Many drugs/antibiotics
This is why some antibiotics are useless for CNS infections.

❤️ Arterial Blood Supply

  1. Internal carotid arteries ascend through neck
  2. Vertebral arteries ascend at back of neck
  3. Each internal carotid → anterior + middle cerebral arteries
  4. Vertebral arteries unite → basilar artery
  5. Basilar divides → posterior cerebral arteries

🔄 The Circle of Willis

💡 Circle of Willis = brain's backup generator: collateral flow reroutes blood.

Circle of Willis: alternate routes for cerebral perfusion.

Arterial anastomosis at base of cerebrum providing alternate blood flow routes if one artery is occluded.

Components

  • R+L anterior cerebral arteries
  • Anterior communicating artery
  • R+L internal carotid arteries
  • R+L posterior cerebral arteries
  • R+L posterior communicating arteries
The brain's built-in redundancy. Why some people survive an occluded artery with minimal deficit.

🔵 Venous Drainage

Cerebral veins drain into dural sinuses, then to heart via internal jugular veins.
