\uD83D\uDC8A The Basic Dosage Formula

Desired \u00F7 Have \u00D7 Vehicle = Dose
  • Desired (D): What the doctor ordered
  • Have (H): What's available on hand
  • Vehicle (V): The form it comes in (mL, tablet, etc.)

Example: Order: 500mg. Available: 250mg/tablet.

500 \u00F7 250 \u00D7 1 = 2 tablets

D/H \u00D7 V. Desired over Have, times Vehicle. Dosage calc exams are usually pass/fail with 100% required!

\uD83D\uDCA7 IV Drip Rate Formula

Volume (mL) \u00D7 Drop Factor \u00F7 Time (min) = gtt/min
  • Drop factor: On IV tubing package. Macro: 10, 15, or 20 gtt/mL. Micro: always 60 gtt/mL.
  • Time: MUST be in minutes (hours \u00D7 60)

Example: 1000mL over 8 hours, drop factor 20 gtt/mL

8 hours = 480 minutes

1000 \u00D7 20 \u00F7 480 = 41.67 \u2248 42 gtt/min

Always convert hours to minutes first. Always round to the nearest whole drop: you can't give half a drop!

\uD83C\uDFAF Practice Problems

  1. Order: 750mg. Available: 250mg/tablet. How many tablets?
    Answer: 750/250 \u00D7 1 = 3 tablets
  2. Order: 0.5g. Available: 250mg/tablet. How many tablets?
    Answer: Convert 0.5g = 500mg. 500/250 \u00D7 1 = 2 tablets
  3. 1000mL IV over 10 hours, drop factor 15 gtt/mL. Rate?
    Answer: 1000 \u00D7 15 \u00F7 600 = 25 gtt/min
  4. Patient weighs 154 lbs. Weight in kg?
    Answer: 154 \u00F7 2.2 = 70 kg
  5. Order: 1.5g. Available: 500mg/5mL. How many mL?
    Answer: 1500/500 \u00D7 5 = 15 mL
