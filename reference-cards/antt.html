WHO 5 Moments of Hand Hygiene

🧱 What is ANTT?

⚠️ If in doubt, it's contaminated — stop, replace and restart.

ANTT is the national standard for aseptic practice in Australian healthcare (ACIPC).

Core rule: Never touch a Key Part or Key Site with anything non-sterile. If in doubt, replace it.

When required: Any procedure bypassing natural defences: IV access, wound care, catheter care, injections.

5 MOMENTS
Before patient • Before aseptic procedure • After body fluid risk • After patient • After surroundings

🔑 Key Parts & Key Sites

⚠️ Key Part touched = REPLACE IT. Never rationalise a shortcut.

🔴 Key Parts (Equipment)

  • Needle and cannula tips
  • Syringe tips and plungers
  • IV hubs and connectors
  • Urinary catheter tip
  • Inner surface of sterile packaging
  • Sterile gauze and dressing materials

🚨 NEVER touch a Key Part with ungloved or non-sterile hands.

🟣 Key Sites (Patient)

  • IV cannula insertion site
  • CVC insertion site
  • Wounds (surgical, traumatic, pressure)
  • Urinary catheter site (meatus)
  • Tracheostomy stoma
  • Drain insertion sites
Minimise exposure time and cover promptly after procedures.

⚖️ Standard vs Surgical ANTT

Standard ANTT

Few small Key Parts, protected individually. Clean gloves.

Examples: IV cannulation, simple wound dressing, SC/IM injections, BGL.

Surgical ANTT

Many Key Parts or too large to protect individually. Sterile gloves + large sterile field.

Examples: Catheter insertion, CVC care, complex wounds, lumbar puncture.

"Can I protect every Key Part individually?" Yes = Standard. No = Surgical.

Choose the smallest aseptic field that safely protects every Key Part.

✅ ANTT Steps

1
Prepare yourself: bare below elbows, no jewellery, short nails.
2
Hand hygiene (Moment 2): ABHR 20-30 sec or soap/water if soiled.
3
Gather equipment: check expiry, packaging integrity.
4
Prepare aseptic field: clean surface, non-touch technique.
5
Don PPE: clean gloves minimum. Surgical = sterile gloves.
6
Protect Key Parts: keep in packaging until use. Never leave uncovered.
7
Perform procedure: if contaminated = replace, don't proceed.
8
Dispose sharps/waste: never resheath needles.
9
Hand hygiene + document.
If contamination occurs: STOP. Replace. Never proceed with a contaminated Key Part.

🧶 WHO 5 Moments

  1. Before touching the patient
  2. Before a clean/aseptic procedure (the critical moment)
  3. After body fluid exposure risk
  4. After touching the patient
  5. After touching patient surroundings

ABHR vs Soap & Water

  • ABHR preferred for most situations (faster, less skin damage)
  • Soap & water required when: hands visibly soiled, after C. difficile, after norovirus
Gloves do NOT replace hand hygiene. HH before donning AND after doffing.
