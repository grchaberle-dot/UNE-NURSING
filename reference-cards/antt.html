\uD83E\uDDF1 What is ANTT?

ANTT is the national standard for aseptic practice in Australian healthcare (ACIPC). It provides a consistent, evidence-based approach to preventing micro-organisms entering a patient during clinical procedures.

The core rule: Never directly touch, or allow anything non-sterile to touch, a Key Part or Key Site. If in doubt, treat it as contaminated and replace it.

When required: Any procedure that bypasses the body's natural defences (skin, mucous membranes): IV access, wound care, catheter care, injections, and more.

\uD83D\uDD11 Key Parts & Key Sites

\uD83D\uDD34 Key Parts (Equipment)

Parts of equipment that, if contaminated, could introduce micro-organisms directly into a patient.

  • Needle and cannula tips
  • Syringe tips and plungers
  • IV catheter hubs and needleless connectors
  • IV line connectors and open ports
  • Urinary catheter tip
  • Inner surface of sterile packaging once opened
  • Sterile gauze and dressing materials

\uD83D\uDEA8 NEVER touch a Key Part with ungloved or non-sterile hands.

\uD83D\uDFE3 Key Sites (Patient)

Portals of entry into the patient's body or a connected invasive device.

  • IV cannula insertion site
  • Central venous catheter insertion site
  • Wounds (surgical, traumatic, pressure injuries)
  • Urinary catheter insertion site (meatus)
  • Tracheostomy stoma
  • Surgical incision sites
  • Drain insertion sites
Key Sites must be kept clean and protected. Minimise exposure time and cover promptly after procedures.

\u2696\uFE0F Standard ANTT vs Surgical ANTT

\uD83E\uDDF1 Standard ANTT

When: Procedures with few, small Key Parts that can each be protected individually. Short-duration.

Aseptic Field: General (clean, non-touch work area). Key Parts protected in their own packaging (Micro Critical Aseptic Field).

Gloves: Clean (non-sterile), as long as Key Parts are not directly touched.

Examples: IV cannulation, IV meds, simple wound dressings, catheter care (not insertion), BGL monitoring, SC/IM injections, NG tube care, blood cultures.

\uD83D\uDC89 Surgical ANTT

When: Procedures with many Key Parts, or Key Parts too large to protect individually. Longer/complex procedures.

Aseptic Field: Large Critical Aseptic Field (sterile drape/surface). All Key Parts placed within it. Entire field maintained sterile.

Gloves: Sterile gloves required (direct Key Part handling within field).

Examples: Urinary catheter insertion, CVC insertion/care, complex wound dressings, chest drain, lumbar puncture, arterial line, surgical procedures.

Decision rule: "Can I protect every Key Part individually without touching it?" If yes = Standard ANTT. If no (too many, too large, too complex) = Surgical ANTT.

\u2705 Performing ANTT: Step by Step

1
Prepare yourself: Clinically clean. No jewellery below elbow, short nails, no nail polish/false nails. Bare below elbows.
2
Hand hygiene (Moment 2): Before clean/aseptic procedure. ABHR 20-30 seconds, or soap and water if hands visibly soiled.
3
Gather and check equipment: Before entering patient zone. Check expiry dates, packaging integrity, all items present.
4
Prepare aseptic field: Clean and dry work surface. Open sterile packaging using non-touch technique. Surgical ANTT: lay out sterile drape for Critical Aseptic Field.
5
Don PPE: Minimum: clean gloves. Add apron, mask, eye protection as required. Surgical ANTT: sterile gloves, sterile gown if indicated.
6
Protect Key Parts throughout: Keep in Micro Critical Aseptic Field until moment of use. Never place on non-sterile surface. Never leave uncovered and unattended.
7
Perform procedure: Maintain aseptic field entire time. If Key Part contaminated = replace it. No shortcuts. Communicate clearly about what is/isn't sterile.
8
Dispose of sharps and waste: Never resheath needles. Sharps directly into sharps container at point of care. Remove PPE in correct order.
9
Hand hygiene (Moment 3 or 4): After body fluid exposure risk or after touching patient. Clean trolley and work area. Document the procedure.
If contamination occurs at any point: STOP. Replace contaminated items before continuing. Never proceed with a contaminated Key Part. Pause and restart > causing a healthcare-associated infection.

\uD83E\uDDF6 WHO 5 Moments of Hand Hygiene

Hand hygiene is the single most effective way to prevent healthcare-associated infections (HAIs).

  1. Before touching the patient: When approaching, before any physical contact
  2. Before a clean/aseptic procedure: Immediately before ANTT (the critical moment)
  3. After body fluid exposure risk: After contact with blood, body fluids, secretions, wound dressings
  4. After touching the patient: After any physical contact, even if gloves were worn
  5. After touching patient surroundings: After touching objects/furniture in patient's environment

ABHR vs Soap & Water

  • ABHR preferred for most situations: faster, less skin damage, more effective against most pathogens. Apply to all surfaces, rub until dry (~20-30 sec).
  • Soap & water required when: hands visibly soiled, after C. difficile contact, after norovirus contact.
Gloves do NOT replace hand hygiene. Perform HH before donning AND after doffing gloves. Over-reliance on gloves is a common student mistake.

\u274C Common Student Mistakes on Placement

\u2717 What Goes Wrong

  • Touching the tip of a needle or syringe, even briefly
  • Placing a sterile item on a non-sterile surface (bed, locker)
  • Reaching ACROSS a sterile field (approach from the side!)
  • Leaving a Key Part uncapped and unattended
  • Using a swab that has fallen or been placed non-sterile
  • Skipping hand hygiene because gloves were worn
  • Not checking expiry dates or packaging integrity
  • Letting a second person contaminate the field unknowingly

\u2713 What to Do Instead

  • Think out loud: narrate your actions during supervised practice
  • Set up your field BEFORE entering the patient zone
  • If unsure whether something is contaminated: treat it as contaminated and replace it
  • Tell your supervisor IMMEDIATELY if you contaminate a Key Part (it's safe to do so)
  • Practice ANTT in clinical skill labs before placement
  • Ask for a second check on your technique (supervisors expect this)
