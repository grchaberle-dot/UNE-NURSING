❗ The Three Ds

⚠️ Delirium = ACUTE onset. Dementia = GRADUAL. Know the difference!

The 3 Ds: know the differences in onset, course, and reversibility.

DELIRIUM = ACUTE 🚨
Delirium comes on fast (hours/days), fluctuates, and is often caused by infection, meds, or metabolic issues. It's a medical emergency. Dementia is slow (months/years) and progressive.

🚨 Delirium

Key Features

  • Acute onset (hours to days)
  • Fluctuating course (lucid intervals)
  • Impaired attention and concentration
  • Disorganised thinking
  • Altered consciousness level
  • Often reversible when cause is treated

Common Causes (Think PINCH ME)

  • Pain
  • Infection (UTI, pneumonia)
  • Nutrition (dehydration, electrolytes)
  • Constipation
  • Hypoxia
  • Medication (polypharmacy, opioids, anticholinergics)
  • Environment (unfamiliar, sleep disruption)

🕒 Dementia

Key Features

  • Gradual onset (months to years)
  • Progressive and irreversible
  • Memory loss (especially recent memory)
  • Language difficulties, disorientation
  • Personality and behavioural changes
  • Impaired ADLs over time

Common Types

  • Alzheimer's disease (most common, ~60-70%)
  • Vascular dementia (stepwise decline)
  • Lewy body dementia (visual hallucinations, parkinsonism)
  • Frontotemporal dementia (personality/behaviour changes early)

📋 Assessment Tools

  • MMSE (Mini-Mental State Examination): screens cognitive function (30-point scale)
  • AMT (Abbreviated Mental Test): quick 10-item screen
  • CAM (Confusion Assessment Method): specifically screens for delirium
  • GDS (Geriatric Depression Scale): screens for depression in older adults
CAM is the gold standard for delirium screening. It checks: acute onset + fluctuating course + inattention + either disorganised thinking or altered consciousness.

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

Delirium Management

  • Treat the underlying cause
  • Reorient frequently (clock, calendar, familiar objects)
  • Maintain sleep-wake cycle
  • Minimise unnecessary moves/changes
  • Avoid restraints where possible
  • Ensure glasses, hearing aids are available
  • Involve family for reassurance

Dementia Care

  • Person-centred approach
  • Simple, clear communication
  • Consistent routines and carers
  • Safe environment (falls prevention, wandering)
  • Support dignity and independence
  • Engage in meaningful activities
  • Support family and carers
