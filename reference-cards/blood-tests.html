Reference ranges vary between pathology labs and differ by patient age, sex, and clinical context. Always interpret results alongside your facility's specific reference intervals and the patient's full clinical picture. When in doubt, escalate.
When interpreting bloods, always consider the TREND (is it improving or deteriorating?) alongside the absolute value.

Clinical Interpretation Tips

  • Haemoglobin <100 g/L: Concerning. Consider transfusion if symptomatic. Investigate cause: GI bleeding, renal, bone marrow dysfunction. Review anticoagulant orders.
  • WCC \u2191 High: Infection or inflammatory response (also order CRP). \u2193 Low: Immunocompromised (handle with care re: infection risk).
  • Platelets \u2193 Low (<150): \u2191 Bleeding risk; flag before invasive procedures. \u2191 High (>450): May indicate clotting risk or inflammatory response.

Interpretation

  • Sodium \u2193: Hyponatraemia. Confusion, may need fluid restriction. Investigate cause (SIADH, diuretics, vomiting).
  • Potassium \u2191\u2193: Out of range = cardiac arrhythmia risk. Priority correction. \u2193 Low: IV KCl mini-bags, oral Slow-K or Chlorvescent. \u2191 High: insulin/glucose infusion or Resonium.
  • Elevated Cr / \u2193 eGFR: Impaired renal function. May be dehydration or nephrotoxic medications. Treat with fluids and/or hold/reduce nephrotoxic drugs (NSAIDs, certain antibiotics).
  • Rhabdomyolysis: If CK (creatine kinase) significantly elevated \u2192 risk of acute kidney injury. Monitor closely.

Key Points

  • INR on Warfarin: Therapeutic goal usually 2-3 (higher for mechanical heart valves). INR >4 \u2192 significant bleeding risk. If supratherapeutic and bleeding: give IV Vitamin K or fresh frozen plasma (FFP).
  • APTT on Heparin: Therapeutic APTT goal is typically 60-100 sec (1.5-2.5\u00D7 normal). Check your facility's heparin infusion protocol for target range.
