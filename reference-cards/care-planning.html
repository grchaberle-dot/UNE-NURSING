✨ The Comprehensive Care Standard

🩺 Start with what matters to this person, not just the diagnosis.

Requires health professionals to identify and address each patient's individual needs through screening, assessment, and risk identification.

  • Deliver quality care based on individualised comprehensive care plan
  • Ensure patient goals, preferences and needs are discussed
  • Identify patients at risk of specific harm
  • Prevent, minimise and manage identified risks
  • Provide comprehensive care, including end of life

📝 Subjective vs Objective Data

Subjective

Reported by patient. Symptoms, feelings, perceptions.

  • "I feel short of breath"
  • Chest pain, fatigue
  • Difficulty sleeping

Objective

Observable or measurable.

  • Low O2 saturation
  • Elevated BP, increased HR
  • Crackles, wheezes
  • Peripheral oedema

🎯 SMART Goals

💡 If you can't measure it or time it, it's not SMART yet.
LetterMeaningExample
SSpecificClearly describe what patient will achieve
MMeasurableObservable or measurable indicator
AAchievableRealistic for patient's condition
RRelevantRelates to the identified problem
TTime-framedWhen the outcome is expected
"Patient will report pain relief within 30 minutes of analgesic" = SMART. "Patient will feel better" = not SMART.
SMART
Specific • Measurable • Achievable • Relevant • Time-framed

A strong goal is specific, measurable, achievable, relevant and time-framed.

👩‍⚕️ Rita Keller: Heart Failure Case Study

Patient Context

Elderly woman, hasn't taken regular meds. SOB at rest, nocturia, waking feeling like drowning.

Objective: Elevated BP, increased HR, low SpO2, crackles, S3, oedema, weight 67→72kg in one month.

Nursing Dx: Ineffective breathing pattern r/t pulmonary congestion.

Interventions: Monitor RR/SpO2, O2 as prescribed, Fowler's position, encourage deep breathing.
