\u2728 The Comprehensive Care Standard

Requires health professionals to identify and address each patient's individual needs through screening, assessment, and risk identification. Care planning occurs in partnership with patient, carers, and family.

  • Deliver quality care based on individualised comprehensive care plan
  • Ensure patient goals, preferences and needs are discussed
  • Identify patients at risk of specific harm
  • Prevent, minimise and manage identified risks
  • Provide comprehensive care, including end of life

\uD83D\uDCDD Subjective vs Objective Data

Subjective

Reported by patient or another person. Symptoms, feelings, perceptions.

  • "I feel short of breath"
  • Chest pain, fatigue
  • Difficulty sleeping
  • Paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnoea

Objective

Observable or measurable through examination, monitoring, diagnostics.

  • Low O2 saturation
  • Elevated BP, increased HR
  • Crackles, wheezes
  • Peripheral oedema, weight gain

\uD83C\uDFAF SMART Goals

LetterMeaningExample
SSpecificClearly describe what patient will achieve
MMeasurableObservable or measurable indicator
AAchievableRealistic for patient's condition
RRelevantRelates to the identified problem
TTime-framedWhen the outcome is expected
"Patient will report pain relief within 30 minutes of analgesic administration" = SMART. "Patient will feel better" = not SMART (not measurable, not time-framed).

\uD83D\uDC69\u200D\u2695\uFE0F Rita Keller: Heart Failure Case Study

Patient Context

Elderly woman presents to ED. Hasn't taken regular meds because daughter (who manages scripts) is away. Usually independent but "this feels different." Called ambulance.

Assessment Findings

Subjective: SOB at rest and exertion, nocturia, waking feeling like drowning, needs to sit up to breathe, weakness, fatigue, possible med interruption.

Objective: Elevated BP, increased HR, low SpO2, crackles on auscultation, S3 heart sound, peripheral oedema, weight 67kg \u2192 72kg in one month.

Priority Problem: Ineffective Breathing Pattern

Nursing Dx: Ineffective breathing pattern related to pulmonary congestion, evidenced by SOB and crackles.

Goal: Improved breathing pattern, maintain prescribed O2 target.

Interventions: Monitor RR/depth/effort, monitor SpO2, administer O2 as prescribed, position high (Fowler's), encourage deep breathing.
