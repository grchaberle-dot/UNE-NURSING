Potassium is the most critical to memorise. Hypo- or hyperkalemia can cause fatal cardiac arrhythmias. Normal K+ is 3.5-5.0. Outside that range = danger zone.

\u2764\uFE0F Key Imbalance Signs

Hypokalemia (K+ < 3.5)

Muscle weakness, fatigue, leg cramps, constipation, cardiac arrhythmias (flattened T wave, U wave on ECG), can be caused by diuretics, vomiting, diarrhoea.

Hyperkalemia (K+ > 5.0)

Muscle weakness, paraesthesia, cardiac arrhythmias (peaked T waves, widened QRS on ECG), can lead to cardiac arrest. Causes: renal failure, tissue damage, excessive supplementation.

Hyponatremia (Na+ < 135)

Confusion, headache, nausea, seizures, muscle cramps. Often from fluid overload (dilutional). "Water intoxication."

Hypernatremia (Na+ > 145)

Thirst, dry mucous membranes, restlessness, irritability, seizures. Usually from dehydration or excessive sodium intake.

Hypocalcemia (Ca2+ < 8.5)

Numbness/tingling (perioral, fingers), muscle spasms, tetany, positive Chvostek's sign (facial twitch when tapped), positive Trousseau's sign (carpopedal spasm with BP cuff).

Hypercalcemia (Ca2+ > 10.5)

"Stones, bones, groans, moans": kidney stones, bone pain, abdominal pain, confusion/depression. Causes: hyperparathyroidism, cancer, immobility.

Chvostek's and Trousseau's signs are exam classics for hypocalcemia. Chvostek = tap the facial nerve, face twitches. Trousseau = inflate BP cuff, hand spasms. Both indicate neuromuscular irritability from low calcium.
