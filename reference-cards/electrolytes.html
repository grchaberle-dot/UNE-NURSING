🧪 Normal Serum Values

Normal ranges: interpret alongside symptoms and local lab ranges.

ElectrolyteNormal RangeLowHigh
Sodium (Na+)135-145 mEq/LHyponatremiaHypernatremia
Potassium (K+)3.5-5.0 mEq/LHypokalemiaHyperkalemia
Calcium (Ca2+)8.5-10.5 mg/dLHypocalcemiaHypercalcemia
Magnesium (Mg2+)1.5-2.5 mEq/LHypomagnesemiaHypermagnesemia
Chloride (Cl-)96-106 mEq/LHypochloremiaHyperchloremia
Phosphate (PO4)2.5-4.5 mg/dLHypophosphatemiaHyperphosphatemia
K+ is the most critical. Hypo- or hyperkalemia can cause fatal arrhythmias. Normal K+ is 3.5-5.0.
💡 Na+ low = confused patient (and potentially seizures).

❤️ Key Imbalance Signs

Tall, Tented T = Hyper-K⁺
Hyperkalaemia classically produces tall, peaked T waves first; widening QRS and severe arrhythmias can follow.

Hypokalemia (K+ < 3.5)

Muscle weakness, fatigue, leg cramps, constipation, cardiac arrhythmias (flattened T wave, U wave).

Hyperkalemia (K+ > 5.0)

Muscle weakness, paraesthesia, peaked T waves, widened QRS, can lead to cardiac arrest.

Hyponatremia (Na+ < 135)

Confusion, headache, nausea, seizures. Often from fluid overload.

Hypernatremia (Na+ > 145)

Thirst, dry mucous membranes, restlessness, seizures. Usually from dehydration.

Hypocalcemia (Ca2+ < 8.5)

Numbness/tingling, muscle spasms, tetany, positive Chvostek's and Trousseau's signs.

Hypercalcemia (Ca2+ > 10.5)

"Stones, bones, groans, moans": kidney stones, bone pain, abdominal pain, confusion.

Chvostek's = tap facial nerve, face twitches. Trousseau's = inflate BP cuff, hand spasms. Both indicate hypocalcemia.
