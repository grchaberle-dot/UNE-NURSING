🔬 Inflammation Process

Acute inflammation: vasodilation, increased permeability, neutrophil migration

🔥 Acute Inflammation

💡 Inflammation is protective, not always bad. It's the body's repair crew.
PRISH
Pain • Redness • Immobility (loss of function) • Swelling • Heat

Inflammation Process Steps

  1. Injury triggers chemical mediators (histamine, prostaglandins)
  2. Vascular response: vasodilation + increased permeability
  3. Cellular response: neutrophils migrate to site (diapedesis)
  4. Phagocytosis: neutrophils and macrophages engulf pathogens
  5. Resolution: tissue repair or fibrosis (scarring)

🩹 Cellular Injury

⚠️ Chronic inflammation = tissue damage over time.

Causes of Cell Injury

  • Hypoxia: most common cause. Ischaemia, respiratory failure, anaemia
  • Physical agents: trauma, burns, radiation, extremes of temperature
  • Chemical agents: toxins, drugs, poisons
  • Infectious agents: bacteria, viruses, fungi, parasites
  • Immune reactions: autoimmune conditions, hypersensitivity
  • Genetic factors: inherited enzyme defects, structural protein abnormalities
  • Nutritional: deficiency or excess

Cell Responses to Injury

  • Atrophy: cell shrinks (disuse, denervation, reduced blood supply)
  • Hypertrophy: cell enlarges (increased workload, e.g. cardiac muscle)
  • Hyperplasia: increased cell number (hormonal stimulation, chronic irritation)
  • Metaplasia: one cell type replaced by another (e.g. smoker's airway)
  • Dysplasia: disordered cell growth (precancerous)
  • Neoplasia: new, uncontrolled growth (benign or malignant)

🛡️ Immunity

💡 Innate = fast but non-specific. Adaptive = slower but targeted and remembers.

Innate (Non-specific)

  • First line: skin, mucous membranes, stomach acid
  • Second line: phagocytes, NK cells, inflammation, fever, complement
  • Fast response, no memory

Adaptive (Specific)

  • Third line: lymphocytes (T cells and B cells)
  • B cells → antibodies (humoral immunity)
  • T cells → cell-mediated immunity (kill infected cells)
  • Slower initial response, develops memory

🦠 Infection & Immunity Concepts

  • Chain of infection: infectious agent → reservoir → portal of exit → mode of transmission → portal of entry → susceptible host
  • Standard precautions: apply to ALL patients regardless of diagnosis
  • Transmission-based precautions: contact, droplet, airborne (added on top of standard)
