\u2728 Mental Health Assessment: First Steps

A mental health assessment is the first step in a person's care journey (Foster et al., 2021). It includes:

  • Personal details, family and medical history
  • How the person came to be referred
  • Presenting problem (expressed by the patient)
  • Comprehensive standardised framework (NMBA standard)
  • Guides the multidisciplinary team toward effective care
A MH assessment is NOT always a structured Q&A. Information can be gained through a therapeutic conversation that is person-centred and empathetic (Foster et al., 2021). Build rapport first.

\uD83D\uDD0D Mental State Examination (MSE)

The MSE gains objective and subjective data about a person's mental state. It forms part of a comprehensive mental health assessment (Volcevska et al., 2023). For NSW nurses, it's a standardised document (NSW Health, 2022).

\uD83D\uDC8A Bipolar Disorder: Key Points

DSM-5 Criteria (Bipolar I, Manic Episode)

  • Persistently elevated/irritable mood lasting 1+ weeks
  • Inflated self-esteem/grandiosity
  • Decreased need for sleep
  • Pressure of speech
  • Flight of ideas/racing thoughts
  • Distractibility
  • Increased goal-directed activity

Pharmacological Treatment

  • Lithium carbonate: First-line for acute mania. Narrow therapeutic range. Monitor for toxicity (thirst, vomiting, polyuria, sweating)
  • Anti-seizure meds: Carbamazepine, sodium valproate, lamotrigine, clonazepam (mood stabilisers)
  • Antipsychotics: Olanzapine, quetiapine, risperidone (for acute mania)
  • Lithium + valproate together has greater efficacy than monotherapy (Shah et al., 2019)
Lithium has a NARROW therapeutic range. Toxicity symptoms: vomiting, diarrhoea, tremors, muscle weakness. Non-compliance is a MAJOR contributing factor to relapse (Semahegn et al., 2020).

\uD83D\uDC69\u200D\u2695\uFE0F Nursing Interventions

1. Therapeutic Relationship

Build trust: the person is more likely to engage with treatment (Foster et al., 2021). Facilitate access to supportive services on discharge. Provide a supportive environment with limited distractions (ideal for mania). Use de-escalation if behaviour becomes dangerous: speak calmly, set clear limits, acknowledge needs.

2. Monitoring

  • Food and fluid intake: Especially if on lithium (electrolyte balance critical)
  • Lithium toxicity signs: Sweating, dehydration, high salt intake, hot weather can trigger toxicity
  • Medication compliance: ~50% of patients with major psychiatric disorders don't adhere (Semahegn et al., 2020). Involve family.

3. Psychosocial Interventions

  • CBT: Recognise and change thoughts/beliefs causing symptoms. Prevents relapse (Chiang et al., 2017)
  • Psychoeducation: Help patient and family recognise early symptoms (Swartz & Suppes, 2019)
  • Mindfulness: Not meditation. "Paying attention on purpose, in the present moment, non-judgmentally" (Kabat-Zinn, 2003). Evidence for stress reduction, pain management, sleep improvement

\uD83D\uDCCB Formulation & Provisional Diagnosis

John Riley Case Formulation

36-year-old male presenting with heightened mood and increased energy (duration: as long as he can remember). Signs of grandiosity, auditory hallucinations, flight of ideas, rapid speech, inability to follow conversation without distraction. Mood and behaviour limiting daily activities. Has not slept in a week. No medications.

Provisional Dx: Acute manic episode, characteristic of Bipolar I Disorder (APA, 2013). Evidenced by: persistently elevated mood (1+ week), inflated self-esteem, decreased sleep, pressure of speech, flight of ideas, distractibility.

Need for referral: Mania with delusions, hallucinations, and lack of insight warrant referral to psychiatrist for further investigation, diagnosis and treatment.
