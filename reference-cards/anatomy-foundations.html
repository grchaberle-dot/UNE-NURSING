\uD83E\uDDEC 4 Tissue Types

Epithelial Tissue

  • Covers body surfaces, lines cavities
  • Functions: protection, absorption, filtration, secretion
  • Avascular (no blood supply; gets nutrients via diffusion)
  • High regeneration rate
  • Types: simple (1 layer), stratified (multiple layers), squamous/cuboidal/columnar

Connective Tissue

  • Most abundant tissue type
  • Functions: support, protection, binding, transport
  • Contains: cells + extracellular matrix (ground substance + fibres)
  • Types: loose (areolar, adipose), dense (tendons, ligaments), cartilage, bone, blood

Muscular Tissue

  • Skeletal: Voluntary, striated, attached to bones
  • Cardiac: Involuntary, striated, only in heart, intercalated discs
  • Smooth: Involuntary, non-striated, in organ walls (GI tract, blood vessels)

Nervous Tissue

  • Found in brain, spinal cord, peripheral nerves
  • Neurons: Conduct electrical impulses
  • Neuroglia: Support cells (don't conduct impulses)
  • Highly specialised, limited regeneration
"Every Never Cries Monday" = Epithelial, Nervous, Connective, Muscular. The four tissue types.

\uD83D\uDD17 Cell Junctions

Junction TypeFunctionWhere Found
Tight JunctionsSeal cells together, prevent leakageBladder lining, intestinal epithelium
Adherens JunctionsLink cells via actin filamentsEpithelial tissues
DesmosomesAnchor cells together (resist tearing)Skin, heart muscle
Gap JunctionsAllow direct cell-to-cell communicationCardiac muscle, smooth muscle, neurons
Gap junctions in cardiac muscle are why the heart beats as a coordinated unit: electrical signals pass directly between cells through these channels.

\uD83D\uDD2C Tissue Repair

Regeneration vs Fibrosis

  • Regeneration: Replacement with same type of cells (ideal outcome)
  • Fibrosis: Replacement with scar tissue (collagen). Functional loss.
  • Tissues with high regeneration: epithelial, bone, blood
  • Tissues with low regeneration: cardiac muscle, nervous tissue
This is why heart attacks cause permanent damage: cardiac muscle can't regenerate. It scars (fibrosis), reducing pumping efficiency.
