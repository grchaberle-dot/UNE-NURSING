\u2696\uFE0F Why Documentation Matters

  • Legal document: Can be subpoenaed in court, reviewed by Coroner, or used in AHPRA investigations. If it isn't documented, legally it didn't happen.
  • AHPRA investigations: Poor or falsified documentation can lead to notifications, conditions on registration, or cancellation.
  • Continuity of care: The next nurse, doctor, or AHP needs your notes to provide safe care.
  • NSQHS Standard 6: Clinical records must be accurate, timely, and accessible (ACSQHC).
  • Clinical safety: Documentation omissions are a leading contributor to adverse events and near-misses.

\uD83C\uDDE6\uD83C\uDDFA Golden Rules

  • Black pen only
  • Date and time every entry
  • Sign with full name and designation
  • Never erase: draw a single line through errors and initial
  • Never document in advance
  • Avoid vague language ("patient seems comfortable"): be specific and objective

\uD83D\uDCCB The CHIMNEOPS Domains

Document only the domains relevant to your patient each shift. Not every domain applies every time. But use this as your mental checklist so nothing gets missed.

C \u2014 Cognitive

Orientation, GCS, confusion, anxiety, behaviour

e.g. Patient alert and oriented to time, place, and person. GCS 15/15. No signs of confusion or agitation noted.

H \u2014 Hygiene

Self-care ability, assisted wash, oral care, grooming

e.g. Patient attended to personal hygiene independently. Oral care performed. Skin clean and dry.

I \u2014 Integrity (Skin)

Wounds, pressure areas, bruising, IV sites, oedema

e.g. Skin intact. No redness over pressure areas. Surgical wound R) abdomen: dressing dry and intact, no signs of infection. IV cannula R) forearm: site patent, no phlebitis.

M \u2014 Mobility

Ambulation, falls risk, aids, assistance level

e.g. Patient mobilised to bathroom with standby assist x1. Falls risk score 12 (high). Non-slip footwear worn. Bed in lowest position.

N \u2014 Nutrition & Hydration

Oral intake, IV fluids, swallowing, diet type, nausea

e.g. Patient tolerated full diet. Ate approximately 75% of lunch. Encouraged oral fluids: intake approx. 600 mL this shift. No nausea or vomiting.

E \u2014 Endocrine

BGL, diabetes management, hormonal concerns

e.g. BGL checked pre-meal: 6.2 mmol/L. Insulin administered as charted. No hypoglycaemic episodes noted.

E \u2014 Elimination

Urine output, bowel, catheter, continence

e.g. Voided x2 this shift: approximately 350 mL, clear yellow urine. Last bowel motion documented yesterday. No IDC in situ.

O \u2014 Observation

Vital signs: T, HR, BP, RR, SpO2, BGL, pain score

e.g. T 36.8\u00B0C, HR 78 bpm (regular), BP 128/76 mmHg, RR 16/min, SpO2 98% on RA. Pain score 2/10. Observations within normal limits.

P \u2014 Psychosocial

Mood, anxiety, family, social supports, consent, capacity

e.g. Patient appears anxious regarding upcoming procedure. Provided information and reassurance. Family present and supportive. Social work referral discussed with RN in charge.

S \u2014 Safety & Comfort

Pain management, positioning, rest, sleep, environment, call bell

e.g. Patient reports pain 3/10 in operative site. Analgesia administered as charted at 1200: effective, pain reduced to 1/10. Repositioned 2-hourly. Call bell within reach. Bed in lowest position, brakes engaged.

\uD83D\uDC8A Additional Domains

Medication

Meds given, refused, held, reactions, education

e.g. All charted medications administered as prescribed. Patient educated regarding new antibiotic: purpose and possible side effects explained. No adverse reactions noted.

Environment

Safety, call bell, bed position, equipment, hazards

e.g. Environment safe and tidy. Call bell within reach. Non-slip mat in place. No hazards identified.

Plan

Ongoing care, referrals, reviews, goals for next shift

e.g. Continue current care plan. Monitor vital signs 4-hourly. Physio review booked for 0900 tomorrow. Wound review by CNS on next shift. Encourage oral fluids. Family meeting arranged for 1600.

\u2705 How to Use CHIMNEOPS on Placement

  1. Before your shift: Print or memorise the CHIMNEOPS headings. Keep the acronym on a notepad in your pocket.
  2. During handover: Jot dot-point notes under each relevant domain as you receive handover.
  3. During the shift: Document contemporaneously (in real-time, not at the end of the shift). Note times.
  4. At end of shift: Review your notes against the CHIMNEOPS checklist. Did you miss any relevant domain?
  5. Before handover: Structure your verbal handover around the same domains. This builds consistency.
CHIMNEOPS + ISBAR = your placement documentation and communication toolkit. CHIMNEOPS for written notes, ISBAR for verbal handover and escalation. Learn both and you'll look like you know exactly what you're doing from day one.
