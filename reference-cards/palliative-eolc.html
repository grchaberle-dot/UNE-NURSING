💜 What is Palliative Care?

❤️ Palliative ≠ giving up. It's quality of life.

An approach that improves quality of life for patients and families facing life-limiting illness. Focuses on preventing and relieving suffering through early identification, assessment, and treatment of pain and other physical, psychosocial, and spiritual problems.

  • Neither hastens nor postpones death
  • Integrates psychological and spiritual aspects of care
  • Offers support to help patients live as actively as possible
  • Helps the family cope during illness and bereavement
  • Uses a team approach to address needs
  • Enhances quality of life and may positively influence disease course
  • Applicable early in illness, alongside curative treatments

Palliative care is not either/or: it runs alongside curative care from diagnosis.

🌟 Key Principles

👨‍👩‍👧 Family is the unit of care.

Comfort Over Cure

  • Symptom management is the priority
  • Pain, nausea, dyspnoea, anxiety
  • "What matters most to you?"

Holistic Assessment

  • Physical symptoms
  • Psychological wellbeing
  • Social and family needs
  • Spiritual/existential concerns
  • Cultural considerations

📊 Pain Assessment in Palliative Care

📝 Use OLDCARTS for a thorough pain history.
OLDCARTS
Onset • Location • Duration • Character • Aggravating factors • Relieving factors • Timing • Severity

WHO Pain Ladder

  1. Step 1: Non-opioid (paracetamol, NSAIDs) ± adjuvants
  2. Step 2: Weak opioid (codeine, tramadol) + non-opioid ± adjuvants
  3. Step 3: Strong opioid (morphine, fentanyl, oxycodone) + non-opioid ± adjuvants
The ladder goes UP for escalating pain, but you can start at any step based on severity. "By the clock, by the ladder, by the mouth."

🙏 End-of-Life Care

Signs of Approaching Death

  • Reduced consciousness and responsiveness
  • Reduced oral intake (food and fluid)
  • Changes in breathing pattern (Cheyne-Stokes, noisy/rattling)
  • Peripheral cyanosis, mottling
  • Cool extremities
  • Reduced urine output
  • Restlessness or agitation

Nursing Care at End of Life

  • Prioritise comfort: regular mouth care, positioning, pain relief
  • Cease unnecessary observations, blood tests, interventions
  • Continue essential medications (usually SC route)
  • Communicate clearly with family: explain what's happening
  • Facilitate family presence and rituals
  • Respect advance care directives
  • Provide emotional and spiritual support
  • Post-death care: body care, documentation, family support

📜 Advance Care Planning

  • Advance Care Directive (ACD): Legal document stating preferences for future care
  • Enduring Power of Attorney (EPOA): Appoints someone to make decisions if the person cannot
  • Goals of Care: Documented discussion between patient, family, and team about treatment boundaries
  • ACDs are legally binding in most Australian states
  • Always check for an ACD on admission
Advance care planning conversations should happen EARLY, not in crisis. Ask: "Has anyone talked to you about what you'd want if you became too unwell to speak for yourself?"

🧑‍⚕️ Bereavement Support

  • Grief is individual: no "right" way to grieve
  • Offer condolences, presence, and practical support
  • Provide information about support services (counselling, peer groups)
  • Follow up with family after death (some services send a card or call)
  • Debrief with your team: caring for dying patients is emotionally demanding
