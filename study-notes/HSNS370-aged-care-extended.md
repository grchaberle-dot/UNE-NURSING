## Global Ageing & Australia's Older Population

Australia has the 5th highest male life expectancy and 8th highest female (AIHW, 2018). In 1890 male life expectancy was 47.2 and female 50.8; by 2016 it had risen to 80.4 and 84.6 respectively. The 2011 Census found 3 million Australians over 65 (1.4 million men, 1.6 million women, 0.7% Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander). One in seven Australians is 65 or older.

For Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples, the age of 50 is classified as "older person" due to the life-expectancy gap.

The UN (2019) projected that population ageing will be one of the most significant social transformations of the 21st century, with implications for labour and financial markets, housing, transport, social protection, family structures and intergenerational ties. By 2050, all regions except Africa will have approximately 25% of their population aged 60 and above.

## Ageism, Stereotypes & the Royal Commission

### Stereotypes of Ageing
Stereotypes are unchallenged myths or oversimplified beliefs. Common myths include that old age begins at 65 (arbitrary pension age), most ageing adults live in nursing homes (only about 6%), most are sick (67.4% of 75+ report good health), old age means mental deterioration (not true), and all old people are incontinent (not a normal part of ageing).

### Ageism
Coined by Butler (1968): "the prejudices and stereotypes that are applied to older people sheerly on the basis of their age." Ageism takes the form of prejudicial attitudes, discriminatory practices and organisational policies. Western cultures tend to hold primarily negative stereotypes, while Eastern cultures often hold more positive views.

### Royal Commission into Aged Care Quality and Safety
Established October 2018, inquiry ran January 2019 to April 2020. Key findings: elder abuse, overuse of psychotropic drugs, poor nutrition, staff retention problems, approximately 80% of dementia residents on antidepressants or sedatives as first-line treatment.

## Community & Residential Aged Care

### Community Services
**CHSP:** Basic entry-level for 65+ (50+ for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples). Supports independence, social connection and avoidance of early residential admission.

**Home Care Packages Program:** For complex care needs at home, including household tasks, equipment, home modifications, personal care and clinical nursing care.

**ACFI:** Assesses residents in permanent residential care across ADLs, cognition/behaviour and complex health care.

### Residential Aged Care
Provides permanent accommodation and short-term respite. In 2016-17, 97% of residents were aged 65+, with 232,000 in permanent residential care.

### RN Role in RACFs
RNs provide nursing leadership and clinical supervision, managing complex health needs using person-centred care. Key issues: frailty, falls risk, skin integrity, self-esteem and independence.

### Dementia Care (BPSD Management)
- Wandering: designate safe spaces
- Sundowning: avoid late afternoon activities
- Anxiety/agitation: reassure, remove cause
- Delusions: do not argue, maintain familiar environment
- Aggression: identify triggers, do not restrain
- Disinhibited behaviour: gentle reminders, redirect

## Positive & Healthy Ageing

Ageing should be viewed from a positive and healthy perspective. Nurses educate older people about health and wellness and empower them with problem-solving strategies. Quality of life in aged care includes choice and control, community involvement, physical and cognitive support, comfort and personal care, and creating a sense of normality.
