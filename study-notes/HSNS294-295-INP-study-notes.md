## Clinical Reasoning & Safe Escalation

### Failure to Rescue
Clinical deterioration not recognised or managed early enough. Contributing factors: infrequent/inaccurate observations, failure to recognise trends, limited knowledge of deterioration, attributing symptoms to existing diagnoses, inadequate communication, hierarchical barriers.

### Clinical Reasoning Cycle (8 Steps)
1. Consider the situation (patient, context, baseline, concerns)
2. Collect information (history, meds, allergies, obs, pathology)
3. Process information (interpret, discriminate, relate, infer)
4. Identify problems (actual and potential)
5. Establish goals (SMART)
6. Take action (nursing and collaborative interventions)
7. Evaluate (compare with baseline/expected)
8. Reflect (learning needs, improvements)

### ABCDE Assessment
- **Airway:** Patency, obstruction, secretions. Deterioration: stridor, gurgling
- **Breathing:** RR, work of breathing, SpO2. Deterioration: tachypnoea, hypoxaemia, accessory muscles
- **Circulation:** Pulse, BP, cap refill, UO. Deterioration: tachycardia, hypotension, cool skin
- **Disability:** LOC, GCS, pupils, BSL. Deterioration: confusion, seizure
- **Exposure:** Temperature, skin, wounds. Deterioration: hypothermia, fever, pressure injury

### ISBAR Communication
Identify, Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation

## Perioperative Nursing

### Preoperative Phase
- Confirm identity (3 identifiers), procedure, site, consent
- Full system assessment and investigations
- Education about OT, anaesthesia, recovery, pain management
- Skin prep: clip not shave; VTE prevention
- Informed consent: if patient cannot explain procedure, escalate

### Intraoperative Phase
- Maintain asepsis, sterile field, surgical counts
- Safe positioning, prevent hypothermia
- Recognise malignant hyperthermia: rapid hyperthermia, tachycardia, rigidity, acidosis

### Postoperative/PACU Care
- Structured handover + ABCDE
- Monitor for opioid respiratory depression, haemorrhage, shock
- Multimodal pain management
- Early mobilisation, deep breathing, VTE prevention

### Complications
Hypoxaemia, atelectasis, pneumonia, PE, haemorrhage, DVT, ileus, urinary retention, wound infection, dehiscence, evisceration, delirium

## Haematology & Oncology Nursing

### Cancer Classifications
Carcinoma (epithelial), sarcoma (connective tissue), myeloma (plasma cells), leukaemia (blood/marrow), lymphoma (lymphatic). TNM staging: T=tumour, N=nodes, M=metastasis.

### Treatment Modalities
Surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, targeted therapy, immunotherapy, hormonal therapy, stem-cell transplantation

### Oncological Emergencies
- **Tumour lysis syndrome:** Hyperkalaemia, hyperphosphataemia, hyperuricaemia, hypocalcaemia, AKI
- **Neutropenic sepsis:** Temp >=38C in neutropenic patient. IV antibiotics within 30 minutes
- **Spinal cord compression:** Pain, weakness, sensory changes

### Safe Cytotoxic Care
Use PPE for preparation, administration and disposal. Follow local policy for spills and waste.

### Blood Transfusion Safety
Confirm identity, blood group, compatibility with another practitioner. Observe closely first 15 minutes.
