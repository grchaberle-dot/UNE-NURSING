# IV Therapy, Medication Administration and Nursing Calculations

**Source:** Southern Cross University, Formation Based Learning: Chronic Illness and Nursing Care (Studocu). Australian.

---

## Why IV Requires Extra Care

Intravenous medications enter the bloodstream rapidly and directly. This makes the route useful when a rapid effect is required, but it also means errors and adverse reactions can occur quickly.

Nurses must use accurate calculations, aseptic technique, patient identification, allergy checking, medication checks, clinical assessment and ongoing monitoring.

> **Safety-critical:** follow the current medication chart, local policy, scope of practice and institutional procedure. If an order is unclear, incomplete, unsafe or inconsistent with the patient's condition, **do not administer** until it has been clarified.

---

## Five Rights of Medication Administration

1. Right person
2. Right medication
3. Right dose
4. Right time
5. Right route

For intravenous therapy, additional checks are essential: correct **fluid, volume, rate, additives, compatibility, expiry date, administration set, access device and pump settings**.

---

## Components of a Valid Medication Order

1. Patient's full name
2. Date and time the order was written
3. Name of the medication
4. Medication dose
5. Frequency of administration
6. Route of administration
7. Signature or authorised identification of the prescriber

> If any of those seven is missing, the order is not valid. Clarify first.

---

## Types of Intravenous Administration

- **IV push or bolus:** medication administered directly into the intravenous access device
- **Intermittent IV infusion:** medication infused over a specified period, for example through a burette or piggyback system
- **Continuous IV infusion:** fluid or medication infused continuously at a prescribed rate

## Safe IV Fluid Administration Sequence

### Before leaving the patient

1. Perform hand hygiene
2. Introduce yourself and explain why you are attending
3. Ask the patient to **state** their full name and date of birth
4. Check the medical record number on the identification band against the medication chart
5. Check the medication chart for a valid order and any additional medications or interactions
6. Ask about allergies
7. Ask about relevant health conditions, recent vomiting, symptoms and other factors that may affect treatment
8. Explain the therapy, including what will happen, expected duration, and how the patient can call for assistance
9. Obtain informed consent

> Ask them to **state** their name. Never read it to them and ask them to agree. Confused or unwell patients will say yes to anything.

### Preparation in the medication room

1. Perform hand hygiene again after leaving the bedside
2. Recheck that the order is legal, complete, clinically appropriate and consistent with the care plan
3. Select the correct fluid and confirm expiry date, solution, additives and volume against the order
4. Select the appropriate administration set. If more than one bag or an additional medication infusion is required, obtain compatible equipment such as a double-bung system where indicated
5. Prepare the prescribed flush using **aseptic non-touch technique (ANTT)**
6. Use a syringe of normal saline to assess patency when required by policy or the order
7. Label the prepared flush according to policy
8. Place the flush and medication or fluid aside for an independent second-nurse check

### Preparing the flush

- Wear non-sterile gloves when handling IV access devices or IV medications, as required by local policy
- Open the syringe and drawing-up needle using ANTT
- Draw up the prescribed amount of normal saline
- Remove air bubbles by holding the syringe upright, tapping gently and expelling air carefully
- Do not contaminate the syringe, needle, access port or other sterile components
- Dispose of sharps immediately into an approved sharps container

> **Safety-critical:** do not reach into a kidney dish or handle a contaminated needle unsafely. Use the approved recapping or disposal method specified by local policy. Many settings prohibit recapping needles.

### Independent double-check

A second nurse should independently verify:

- Correct patient
- Medication or fluid order
- Correct medication and solution
- Correct dose or volume
- Expiry date
- Route and prescribed rate
- Compatibility and additives
- Correct flush
- Pump settings and administration equipment

> **Independent** means the second nurse checks against the order and does the calculation themselves, not simply agreeing with you.

### At the patient's bedside

1. Perform hand hygiene
2. Reconfirm the patient's identity using full name, date of birth and medical record number
3. Recheck allergies
4. Explain the flush and IV therapy, including possible coolness, pain or stinging
5. Ask the patient when the cannula was inserted and whether it is painful
6. Inspect the cannula site for redness, swelling, heat, tenderness, leakage, bleeding, discharge or other signs of complications
7. Check that the cannula is secure, dry, intact and labelled with the insertion date where required
8. Clean the access site with an appropriate alcohol swab and allow it to dry fully
9. Flush the cannula according to local policy. Stop and assess if the patient reports pain, or if resistance, swelling, leakage or other abnormality occurs

---

## NEVER FORCE A FLUSH

**Never force a flush against resistance.**

Pain, swelling, redness, coolness, leakage, resistance or a damp dressing may indicate:

- **Infiltration**
- **Extravasation**
- **Phlebitis**
- **Infection**
- **Device malfunction**

**Action:** stop the infusion, assess the site, escalate according to policy.

> This is the single most important safety rule on this page. Forcing a flush can push a clot into circulation or force medication into tissue.

## Priming and Commencing the IV Line

1. Check the fluid bag again against the legal order
2. Inspect the bag for damage, cloudiness, particles, leakage or other visible faults
3. Place the bag on the IV stand
4. Open the administration set without allowing it to touch the floor or contaminated surfaces
5. Close the roller clamp and other clamps **before** spiking the bag
6. Remove protective covers without contaminating the spike or connection points
7. Spike the bag using the appropriate technique
8. Squeeze the drip chamber to approximately **halfway full** so the drip rate remains visible
9. Open the relevant clamps slowly and prime the line until fluid reaches the distal end
10. Close the roller clamp once priming is complete
11. Connect the line to the cannula using aseptic technique
12. Place the tubing into the infusion pump using correct orientation and colour-coded locations
13. Open the prescribed clamps and enter the ordered volume and rate
14. Ask a second nurse to verify patient, order, medication or fluid, volume, rate, expiry date and pump settings
15. Confirm allergies and obtain agreement to commence therapy
16. Start the infusion according to the pump procedure and verify the line is open and running correctly
17. Secure the line with tape without obscuring the insertion site

Tell the patient to use the call bell before getting out of bed, because the IV pole may need to accompany them. Confirm the call bell is within reach and the patient is comfortable.

> Drip chamber halfway full. Too full and you cannot see the drip rate. Too empty and you risk air entering the line.

---

## Monitoring and Documentation

After commencing the infusion, return regularly to assess the patient and the IV system. Check insertion site, line, pump, fluid remaining, prescribed rate, patient response and any signs of complications.

### Documentation should include

- Two-nurse signatures where required
- Commencement of the bag on the IV fluid or fluid-balance chart
- Date and time the infusion commenced
- Type and volume of fluid
- Prescribed infusion rate
- Any additives
- Assessment findings for the cannula site
- Patient response and any adverse effects
- Ongoing fluid input and output as required
- Actions taken if complications occur
- Commencement of a new bag in the progress notes where required

At completion: dispose of sharps safely, clean the bedside and treatment area, perform hand hygiene, ensure the patient knows how to request assistance.

## Unit Conversions for Nursing Calculations

| Measurement | Units |
|---|---|
| Mass | microgram (mcg), milligram (mg), gram (g) |
| Volume | millilitre (mL) |

- Converting from a **larger unit to a smaller unit: MULTIPLY**
- Converting from a **smaller unit to a larger unit: DIVIDE**

> **Safety-critical:** always write units clearly and perform a final reasonableness check. Confusing micrograms, milligrams and grams can cause a serious medication error. Each step is a factor of 1000.

---

## Rounding for Nursing Calculations

Inspect the digit immediately to the **right** of the place you are rounding to:

- If that digit is **5 or greater**, round up
- If that digit is **less than 5**, leave the retained digit unchanged

**Worked example:** rounding 1.491 to a whole number gives **1**, because the first decimal digit (4) is less than 5.
