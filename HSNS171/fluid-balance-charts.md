# Fluid Balance Charts

**Source:** Studocu AU, Fluid Balance Charts Summary Note. Australian university context.

---

## What Is Fluid Balance?

Fluid balance is the relationship between the amount of fluid **entering** and **leaving** the body. Maintaining this balance is essential for normal metabolic processes and overall patient health.

---

## Importance in Nursing Assessment

To assess a patient's fluid balance accurately, nurses need to understand:

- The body's **fluid compartments**
- How fluid **moves between them**

Fluid balance charts help monitor fluid intake and output and identify potential changes in hydration status.

> You cannot interpret a fluid balance chart without understanding ICF, ECF and interstitial fluid. The chart is just numbers until you know where the fluid goes.

---

## Dehydration

Dehydration is a loss of body mass caused by fluid loss.

### Symptoms

- **Impaired cognitive function**
- Headaches
- Fatigue
- Dry skin

### Severe progression

Severe dehydration may progress to:

1. **Hypovolaemic shock**
2. **Organ failure**
3. **Death**

Early recognition and appropriate intervention are essential.

> Impaired cognition is listed first for a reason. Confusion in an older patient is very often dehydration, not dementia. Check the fluid balance before assuming anything else.

---

## Assessing Hydration Status

Three key areas contribute to the assessment of fluid balance and hydration:

1. **Clinical assessment:** observing the patient for signs and symptoms of dehydration or fluid imbalance
2. **Body weight:** monitoring changes that may indicate fluid loss or gain
3. **Urine review, fluid-balance review and blood chemistry:** evaluating urine output, recorded intake and output, and relevant blood results

> Daily weights are the most reliable measure of fluid shift. One kilogram of weight change is roughly one litre of fluid, far more accurate than eyeballing it.

---

## Documentation Challenges

Fluid balance documentation is often **inadequate or inaccurate**. Common contributing factors:

- **Staff shortages**
- **Insufficient training**
- **Lack of time**

> These are system problems, not individual laziness. The fix is usually a workflow change rather than telling nurses to try harder.

---

## Why Accurate Recording Matters

Accurate and complete recording is important because it supports:

- **Reliable assessment** - you cannot assess what was not recorded
- **Timely recognition of deterioration** - trends over hours reveal problems a single observation misses
- **Safe patient care**

> A half-completed fluid chart is arguably worse than none, because it looks like data and gets trusted like data.
