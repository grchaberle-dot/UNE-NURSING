# PSIO215 Pharmacology: Reference Notes

> **International nursing pharmacology notes (structure and mnemonics reference only, not for app)**

## Fundamentals
- **Pharmacodynamics:** what drug does to body (mechanism of action)
- **Pharmacokinetics:** what body does to drug (ADME)
- **Pharmacotherapeutics:** choosing the best drug

## Phases of Drug Therapy
1. **Pharmaceutic:** disintegration and dissolution
2. **Pharmacokinetic:** absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion
3. **Pharmacodynamic:** drug-receptor interaction, onset, peak, duration

## ADME
### Absorption
- Routes: oral, sublingual, IM, SC, IV, topical, inhalation
- First-pass effect: hepatic enzyme inactivation before systemic circulation
- Bioavailability: % of administered dose reaching systemic circulation

### Distribution
- Protein binding: bound portion inactive, free portion active
- Blood-brain barrier: lipid-soluble drugs cross; most antibiotics cannot
- Placenta: drugs readily pass through

### Metabolism
- Mainly liver (hepatic enzymes)
- Half-life: time for 50% elimination
- First order: proportional to concentration; Zero order: constant rate

### Excretion
- Kidneys (main): free, water-soluble, unbound drugs
- Acidic urine for weak bases; alkaline urine for weak acids

## Drug Interactions
- Additive (1+1=2), Synergistic (1+1>2), Antagonistic (one blocks another)
- Therapeutic index: narrow = higher toxicity risk

## Antimicrobials
### Aminoglycosides (gentamicin, tobramycin, amikacin)
- MOA: bactericidal, bind 30S ribosomal subunit, inhibit protein synthesis
- Must give IM/IV; renal excretion
- **Ototoxicity** (irreversible): auditory (amikacin/kanamycin), vestibular (gentamicin/tobramycin)
- **Nephrotoxicity** (reversible): monitor BUN/creatinine
- Nursing: audiogram, peak/trough levels, hydrate well, slow IV

### Penicillins
- MOA: bactericidal, inhibit cell wall synthesis via PBPs
- Narrow spectrum: Pen G (streptococci, syphilis)
- Penicillinase-resistant: nafcillin, oxacillin (staph infections)
- Wider spectrum: ampicillin, amoxicillin (with clavulanic acid = Co-Amoxiclav)
- Allergy: 5-10% cross-reactivity with cephalosporins

### Cephalosporins (5 generations)
| Gen | Key Feature | Example |
|-----|-------------|--------|
| 1st | Gram (+) cocci, surgical prophylaxis | Cefazolin, cephalexin |
| 2nd | Extended gram (-) | Cefuroxime, cefaclor |
| 3rd | Gram (-) rods, crosses BBB | Ceftriaxone, ceftazidime |
| 4th | Gram (+) of 1st + gram (-) of 3rd | Cefepime |
| 5th | MRSA activity | Ceftaroline |

### Vancomycin
- MOA: inhibits peptidoglycan chain elongation
- For MRSA, PRSP, C. difficile (oral)
- **Red Man Syndrome:** rapid IV infusion -> histamine release
- Monitor: renal function, ototoxicity

## Antihypertensives
### ACE Inhibitors (-pril)
- MOA: block angiotensin I -> II conversion
- SE: dry cough, hypotension, hyperkalaemia, angioedema
- CI: pregnancy, renal artery stenosis

### ARBs (-sartan)
- Selectively bind angiotensin II receptors
- Used when ACE inhibitors not tolerated (no cough)

### CCBs (amlodipine, diltiazem, verapamil)
- MOA: prevent Ca2+ entry into myocardium/vascular smooth muscle
- Uses: angina, hypertension, atrial fibrillation
- SE: headache, peripheral oedema, constipation, bradycardia

### Beta-Blockers (-olol)
- MOA: block beta-1 (heart) and/or beta-2 (lungs) receptors
- CI: asthma, heart block, COPD, diabetes (masks hypoglycaemia)
- **Withhold if pulse <60 or SBP <90**
- BLOCKER mnemonic: Bradycardia, Lipidaemia, brOnchospasm, CHF, Konstriction, Exhaustion, Reduces hypoglycaemia recognition

### Alpha-Blockers (-sin)
- MOA: block alpha-1 receptors -> vasodilation
- SE: first-dose syncope, orthostatic hypotension
- Uses: BPH (doxazosin, terazosin)

## Cardiac Glycosides (Digoxin)
- MOA: inhibits Na-K pump -> increased intracellular Ca
- (+) inotropic, (-) chronotropic, (-) dromotropic
- Uses: CHF, atrial fibrillation
- **Therapeutic range: 0.5-2 ng/mL** (narrow margin of safety)
- Toxicity: anorexia, N/V, bradycardia, yellow/green halos, confusion
- Antidote: digoxin immune Fab
- **Check apical pulse for 1 full minute before giving**
- Hypokalemia increases toxicity risk

## Anti-Anginals (Nitrates)
- MOA: vein dilation (decrease preload) + artery dilation (decrease afterload)
- SL: every 5 min x 3 doses; burning = potent; store dark/dry; expires 6 months
- Patch: remove after 12 hours; rotate sites; no defibrillator paddles over patch
- IV: glass bottles, infusion pump
- **Withhold: BP <90/60, HR <60**

## Anti-Arrhythmics
| Class | MOA | Example |
|-------|-----|--------|
| I (Na channel) | Block Na channels | Quinidine, procainamide |
| II (Beta) | Beta-blocker | Propranolol, esmolol |
| III (K channel) | Prolong repolarisation | Amiodarone |
| IV (Ca channel) | Block Ca channels | Verapamil, diltiazem |

## Diuretics
### Thiazide (hydrochlorothiazide)
- Site: distal tubule; mild-moderate HPN
- SE: hypoK, hyperglycaemia, hyperuricaemia, hypercalcaemia

### Loop (furosemide/Lasix)
- Site: Loop of Henle; rapid diuresis
- SE: hypoK, hypoCa, ototoxicity (IV), dehydration
- **Give slow IV (2 min) to prevent hearing loss**

### Osmotic (mannitol)
- Site: glomerulus/tubule; increased ICP, oliguria
- Check for crystallisation; use IV filter; infuse 30-60 min

### Potassium-Sparing (spironolactone)
- Site: distal tubule; aldosterone antagonist
- SE: hyperK (peaked T wave on ECG)
- **Avoid K-rich foods and K supplements**

## Key Electrolyte Foods
- **K-rich:** banana, avocado, broccoli, dried fruit, orange, nuts, potato, prunes, tomato
- **Na-rich:** canned/processed foods, fast food, ketchup
- **Ca-rich:** dairy, broccoli, seafood, spinach
