# PSIO120/215 Nephrology & Renal Physiology: Reference Notes

> **International nephrology notes (structure reference only, not for app)**

## Kidney Structure
- Functional unit: nephron (glomerulus + tubular system)
- Cortical nephrons (85%) and juxtamedullary nephrons (15%)

## Urine Formation
1. **Filtration:** plasma filtered at glomerulus into Bowman's capsule
2. **Reabsorption:** 80% water/Na/K/Cl reabsorbed in proximal tubule; Loop of Henle concentrates urine
3. **Secretion:** H+, K+, uric acid, creatinine secreted into tubule

## Nephron Segments
| Segment | Key Function |
|---------|--------------|
| Proximal tubule | Reabsorbs 65% filtered Na, glucose, amino acids, bicarbonate |
| Descending LoH | Water reabsorption (permeable to water) |
| Ascending LoH | Na/Cl reabsorption (impermeable to water) |
| Distal tubule | Fine-tuning of Na, K, H+ under aldosterone/ADH |
| Collecting duct | Final water reabsorption (ADH-dependent) |

## Acid-Base Balance
- Kidneys regulate pH by reabsorbing/generating HCO3- and excreting H+
- Respiratory compensation is rapid; renal compensation takes hours to days

## Common Renal Conditions
### AKI (Acute Kidney Injury)
- Pre-renal (hypoperfusion), intrinsic (tubular/glomerular), post-renal (obstruction)
- Oliguria (<0.5 mL/kg/hr), rising creatinine/urea

### CKD (Chronic Kidney Disease)
- Progressive irreversible nephron loss; GFR staging (G1-G5)
- Complications: anaemia (reduced EPO), bone disease, fluid overload, hyperkalaemia

### Nephrotic Syndrome
- Heavy proteinuria (>3.5g/day), hypoalbuminaemia, oedema, hyperlipidaemia
- Children: minimal change disease most common

### Nephritic Syndrome
- Haematuria, mild proteinuria, hypertension, oliguria
- Post-streptococcal GN in children

## Electrolyte Disorders
| Disorder | Key Features |
|----------|--------------|
| Hyperkalaemia | Peaked T waves, risk of cardiac arrest; treat with calcium gluconate, insulin/dextrose, salbutamol |
| Hypokalaemia | U waves, muscle weakness, cramps; replace K+ |
| Hyponatraemia | Confusion, seizures; assess volume status first |

## Paediatric Nephrology
- UTI: most common bacterial infection in children; E. coli most frequent
- HUS: haemolytic anaemia + thrombocytopaenia + AKI; often post-diarrhoeal (STEC)

## Renal Replacement Therapy
- Haemodialysis: blood filtered through external circuit
- Peritoneal dialysis: peritoneal membrane as filter
- Transplantation: definitive treatment for ESKD
