## Scope

35-page compilation covering core pathophysiology, cardiac conduction and ECGs, dysrhythmias, heart failure, STEMI recognition, cardiac arrest and post-ROSC care, oxygenation, ventilation, capnography, CPAP/PEEP, adult respiratory emergencies and paediatric emergencies.

## High-yield scaffold

- **Core pathophysiology**: homeostasis, inflammation, ischaemia, hypoxia versus hypoxaemia, cellular adaptation, apoptosis versus necrosis, neoplasia and TNM staging.
- **Cardiac conduction**: SA node -> AV node -> Bundle of His -> bundle branches -> Purkinje fibres. AV nodal delay permits ventricular filling.
- **Cardiac output**: heart rate x stroke volume. Preload, afterload and contractility determine stroke volume; Frank-Starling describes the filling-contraction relationship.
- **ECG method**: rate, rhythm, P waves, PR interval, QRS, T waves, origin, rhythm diagnosis. Learn the difference between sinus rhythms, ectopics, AF, flutter, SVT, VT, VF and AV blocks.
- **Heart failure**: left-sided failure backs up into pulmonary circulation; right-sided failure produces systemic venous congestion. RAAS and sympathetic compensation help initially but increase workload over time.
- **STEMI scaffold**: use contiguous leads, reciprocal changes and coronary territories. Inferior patterns may require right-ventricular assessment. Treat this as a recognition framework only.
- **Arrest and ROSC**: shockable versus non-shockable rhythms, 4 Hs and 4 Ts, post-arrest brain injury, myocardial stunning, systemic ischaemia-reperfusion and the need to treat the original cause.
- **Ventilation**: oxygenation is oxygen delivery; ventilation is carbon-dioxide removal. Capnography reflects ventilation and can give indirect information about circulation. Trend the waveform, not just the number.
- **CPAP/PEEP**: positive pressure can recruit alveoli and reduce work of breathing, but requires careful patient selection and monitoring for hypotension, aspiration and barotrauma.
- **Adult respiratory emergencies**: asthma involves bronchoconstriction and inflammation; COPD includes emphysema and chronic bronchitis. Silent chest, exhaustion, cyanosis or altered consciousness are deterioration flags.
- **Paediatrics**: airway anatomy, diaphragm-dependent breathing, heart-rate-dependent cardiac output and small absolute blood volume make early recognition crucial. Use the paediatric assessment triangle as a rapid framework.
