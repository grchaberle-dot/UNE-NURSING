# Studocu Reference: Human Bioscience, Paramedicine, Trauma and Pharmacology

> **UNVERIFIED SCAFFOLD MATERIAL**: These notes came from a non-nursing paramedicine/bioscience source on Studocu. Use them as a concept map only. Verify clinical values, protocols, doses, assessment criteria and Australian practice requirements against UNE materials and current Australian guidance. The source contained omissions and transcription errors.

## Scope

27-page compilation covering human biology, muscle physiology, respiratory and cardiovascular foundations, capnography, neurology, emergencies, paramedic assessment, trauma, burns, environmental injury and medication principles.

## High-yield scaffold

- **Human biology**: structural organisation from chemical to organismal level; homeostasis; tissue types; diffusion, osmosis and tonicity.
- **Muscle physiology**: skeletal, cardiac and smooth muscle; connective-tissue layers; sarcomere bands; excitation-contraction coupling; calcium, troponin, tropomyosin and the cross-bridge cycle; motor-unit recruitment and fatigue.
- **Respiratory anatomy**: upper and lower airway, pleura, lobes, thoracic wall, diaphragm and pressure-volume relationships. Distinguish oxygenation from ventilation.
- **Capnography**: waveform phases, ETCO2 trends, airway obstruction, bronchospasm, CPR quality and sudden sustained rise with ROSC. A number without a waveform and clinical assessment is weak evidence.
- **Cardiovascular/ECG**: chambers, valves, coronary supply, conduction pathway, ECG components and a five-step rhythm approach. Recognise AF, flutter, VT, VF, heart blocks and possible hyperkalaemia changes.
- **Neurological assessment**: CNS/PNS, afferent/efferent pathways, autonomic balance, brain regions, cranial nerves, AVPU, GCS, FAST, ROSIER, pupils and coordination tests.
- **Respiratory emergencies**: asthma, COPD, pneumonia, pulmonary embolism and ARDS. Deterioration signs require escalation and local-protocol management.
- **Cardiovascular emergencies**: ACS, heart failure, tamponade, pericarditis, cardiomyopathy, hypertensive crisis and aortic dissection.
- **Neurological emergencies**: ischaemic versus haemorrhagic stroke, TIA, seizures and neuromuscular disorders. Rapid assessment and transport pathways matter.
- **Endocrine/GI/renal**: hypoglycaemia, DKA, HHS, thyroid/adrenal emergencies, abdominal pain patterns, GI bleeding, renal colic, renal failure and dialysis considerations.
- **Assessment frameworks**: ETHANE, primary survey, airway adjuncts, respiratory examination, SAMPLE, OPQRST and IMIST-AMBO handover.
- **Trauma**: kinetic energy, compression, stretch, shear and cavitation; mechanism of injury; penetrating trauma; spinal injury; neurogenic versus haemorrhagic shock; burns and inhalation injury.
- **Pharmacology**: broad mechanisms and cautions for adrenaline, salbutamol, ipratropium, hydrocortisone, atropine, amiodarone, GTN, aspirin, opioids, naloxone, ketamine, antiemetics and common suffix patterns.

## Theatre nursing relevance

Particularly useful as a future scaffold for PSIO110/120 and PSIO215: muscle contraction and neuromuscular junctions, airway anatomy, respiratory mechanics, capnography, neurological assessment, trauma physiology and medication mechanisms.

## Verification flags

Medication administration, emergency algorithms, dose tables, airway decisions, spinal-motion guidance and numerical thresholds are scope- and protocol-dependent. The original compilation itself identified missing doses and apparent errors. Treat it as a guide for what to investigate, never as a prescribing or clinical practice source.
