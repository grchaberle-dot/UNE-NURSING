1. Critical Thinking

Multi-faceted: not just skills but attitudes, the right approach, reasoning, reflecting and coming to a conclusion. Important for academic, professional and personal life.

3. Critical Reading + Authenticity

  • Analysing information = the starting point of critical reading
  • Read slower; work out what the author wants you to believe and how strong their evidence is
  • Find the author's point, then decide if you agree = reasoning out a conclusion
  • Authenticity: is it what it claims to be? (the "long lost painting found in a shed")
Recommended: Cottrell (2023) Critical thinking skills (4th ed.). Look over Ch 1, skim Ch 2, 6, 7, 8.

4. Fake News, Circular Reporting + Bias

  • Circular reporting can reinforce misinformation
  • Check the bias of the source or commentator (Penn State news-types guide, US, reference only)
  • Ask: Is this true? Can I trust it? Is the evidence complete?
  • We all have biases. Do I agree because it fits my biases, or reject it because it challenges them?
  • We often take things for granted and assume they're correct
Reflect: where do you get news? In-depth or sensational? Reliable enough for practice? How do you judge factual + unbiased?

📚 Library Video: Peer Review

  • Editor → 2+ expert reviewers → check methodology, originality, significance, clarity → author revises → published
  • Passing peer review is not the final statement; it's the start of wider scrutiny
  • Letters to the editor, book reviews, editorials usually aren't peer reviewed
  • Library Search: Peer reviewed journals filter (eye + book symbol)
  • Google Scholar doesn't flag peer review, so not your first choice
  • Ulrichsweb: black-and-white referee jersey = refereed = peer reviewed
  • A thesis is NOT peer reviewed
