1. The Publication / Publisher

  • Scroll down for metadata (info about the article)
  • Who's the publisher? Where are they based?
  • Is there an About Us page? (usually at the bottom of webpages)
  • What do others say about the publication or publisher?
  • Newspaper or journal? Check the Library's Ulrichsweb database
  • Goal: spot any bias. Are they conservative, radical, controversial?
  • Domain clues: .com / .gov / .edu, and .au = Australian
  • How old is the information?

2. The Authors

  • Academics? Professors? PhD students? Journalists? Members of the public?
  • Is the listed uni or organisation reputable, and do they really work there?
  • Where do they get their info? What else have they written?
  • Any political, commercial, religious or other ties that might cause bias?

3. The Content (Journal Article)

  • Peer reviewed? Check Library Search, or look for the black and white referee jersey in Ulrichsweb
  • Info about the authors? An abstract?
  • Well written and presented, or does something feel off?
  • Citations + reference list?
  • Funding info? Conflicts of interest?

4. Defining Reliability & Validity

  • Start with good definitions
  • The Library Subject Guides are a good starting point for dictionaries and encyclopedias
  • Library Search finds books, reference entries and articles to back up your claims
  • A librarian can help you with search terms. The ASO (Students box on the Library homepage) has writing and referencing handouts
