# Musculoskeletal System Overview

**Source:** Healthdirect Australia (healthdirect.gov.au). Australian government source.

---

## The Musculoskeletal System

Bones, muscles and joints make up the musculoskeletal system, along with cartilage, tendons and ligaments. It gives the body structure and support, enables movement, and protects organs.

Around **1 in 3 Australians** have some type of musculoskeletal condition.

### Components

- **Skeleton:** 206 bones in the adult human body
- **Joints:** areas where two bones work together
- **Cartilage:** cushioning covering the ends of bones
- **Ligaments:** tough tissue bands joining **bone to bone**. Strengthen joints
- **Muscles:** over 600 skeletal muscles in the body
- **Tendons:** strong fibrous connective tissue attaching **muscle to bone**

> Ligaments join bone to bone. Tendons tie muscle to bone.

---

## Role of Bones

- Give shape, hold the body upright
- Protect internal organs (heart, liver)
- Help movement
- Made of **collagen** (flexible protein framework) plus **calcium phosphate** (strength and hardness)
- Most growth during childhood and teenage years
- Constantly changing through **remodelling**: bone cells replace old or damaged bone and build new tissue
- **Bone marrow** in the centre makes new blood cells

### The spine

- Central support of the body
- **33 vertebrae** separated by discs
- Carries all nerve signals between brain and body

## Three Types of Muscle

### Skeletal muscle (voluntary)

- Consciously controlled
- Attached to bone by tendons
- Functions: movement, heat generation, organ protection, posture maintenance
- Brain signals the muscle to contract, it shortens and pulls one bone toward another across a joint
- **Work in pairs:** when one shortens, the corresponding muscle lengthens (bicep contracts, tricep lengthens)
- Important role in **blood sugar regulation**: takes up glucose from blood for fuel or storage
- Physical activity maintains or increases strength

### Smooth muscle (involuntary)

- Found inside blood vessels and organs such as the intestines
- Cannot be consciously controlled
- Contracts to move food and stool through the digestive system
- In arteries and veins, helps regulate blood pressure

### Cardiac muscle (involuntary)

- Heart only
- Cannot be consciously controlled
- Contracts to make the heart beat
- Controlled by the sinoatrial node, the heart's inbuilt pacemaker

## Three Types of Joints

### Synovial joints (most common)

- Found in arms and legs
- Bone ends covered with cartilage, separated by a joint cavity
- Joint cavity filled with **synovial fluid**, which lubricates cartilage and provides nourishment
- Ligaments stretch across the joint, connect bones and stabilise movement direction

### Cartilaginous joints

- Found in spine, pelvis, between ribs and sternum
- **More stability, less movement**
- Bones connected by cartilage

### Fibrous joints

- Allow **no movement**, just stability
- Held by strong fibrous connective tissue
- Found in the skull

> More movement means less stability. Synovial joints move freely but dislocate. Fibrous joints never move and never fail.

## Common Bone Conditions

- **Fractures:** broken bones
- **Osteopaenia / osteoporosis:** reduced bone density, making fractures more likely
- **Paget's disease:** weakens and deforms bones
- **Bone cancer:** primary (starts in bone) or secondary (spreads from elsewhere)
- **Rickets:** childhood bone disease from low vitamin D
- **Osteomyelitis:** bone infection, usually bacterial

### Bone health tips

- Calcium-rich diet
- Limit alcohol
- No smoking
- Active lifestyle with weight-bearing and high-impact activities
- Adequate sunshine (vitamin D)
- Healthy weight

> Weight-bearing is the key word. Swimming is excellent cardio but does almost nothing for bone density because there is no load.

## Common Joint Disorders

- **Osteoarthritis:** more common with age. Knees, hips, finger joints, big toe. Wear and tear
- **Rheumatoid arthritis:** autoimmune. The immune system attacks the joint lining
- **Septic arthritis:** infection, usually bacterial
- **Psoriatic arthritis:** inflammatory, affects people with psoriasis
- **Gout:** uric acid crystals in joints, causing pain, redness and inflammation
- **Ankylosing spondylitis:** affects neck, spine and pelvis joints, causing back pain
- **Sprains:** ligaments stretched or torn

> Osteoarthritis is mechanical wear. Rheumatoid is autoimmune attack. Gout is crystals. Three different mechanisms, three different treatments.

---

## Common Muscle Disorders

- **Strains:** muscle overstretched or contracted too quickly, causing partial or complete tear
- **Muscle cramps:** sudden painful contractions
- **Tendonitis:** inflammation or irritation of a tendon
- **Fibromyalgia:** widespread pain, stiffness, extreme fatigue, poor sleep
- **Muscular dystrophies:** genetic disorders causing progressive muscle mass loss and weakness
- **Sarcopenia:** age-related loss of muscle mass and quality
- **Myositis:** autoimmune inflammation of muscle tissue

> Sarcopenia is the one that quietly matters most in older patients. It drives falls, frailty and loss of independence.
