## Nervous System Overview

- **CNS:** brain and spinal cord
- **PNS:** all neural tissue outside the CNS (cranial nerves, spinal nerves, ganglia, peripheral receptors)
- **Afferent** (sensory): carry information toward the CNS
- **Efferent** (motor): carry commands away from the CNS to muscles and glands

### Sensory input types

| Type | Detects |
|---|---|
| Somatic sensory | Touch, pain, temperature, proprioception (skin, muscles, joints) |
| Visceral sensory | Internal organ information (stretch, chemical changes) |

---

## Spinal Cord Anatomy

Cylindrical nervous tissue, a direct continuation of the brainstem. Major communication pathway between brain and body.

- **Ascending pathways** carry sensory information toward the brain
- **Descending pathways** carry motor commands from the brain
- Regions: cervical, thoracic, lumbar, sacral
- The cord is shorter than the vertebral column. It ends around **L1/L2** in adults

### Cauda equina

Below the cord's end, lower spinal nerve roots travel downward within the vertebral canal before exiting. This bundle is the **cauda equina** (horse's tail).

### Spinal cord enlargements

- **Cervical enlargement:** additional neurons for upper limb innervation (brachial plexus)
- **Lumbar enlargement:** additional neurons for lower limb innervation (lumbosacral plexuses)

### Grey and white matter

| Structure | Function |
|---|---|
| Posterior (dorsal) horn | Processes sensory / afferent information |
| Anterior (ventral) horn | Contains motor neurons to skeletal muscles |
| Lateral horn | Contains autonomic (sympathetic) neurons. Present in thoracic and upper lumbar regions |
| White matter columns | Ascending sensory tracts plus descending motor tracts |

## Spinal Nerves and Roots

- **Dorsal root:** carries sensory axons into the spinal cord
- **Dorsal root ganglion:** contains sensory neuron cell bodies (pseudounipolar neurons)
- **Ventral root:** carries motor fibres out of the spinal cord (somatic and autonomic)
- **Spinal nerve:** formed when dorsal and ventral roots unite. Always **mixed** (sensory plus motor)

After forming, each spinal nerve divides into:

- **Dorsal ramus:** smaller. Innervates back structures
- **Ventral ramus:** larger. Innervates the anterior body and forms major nerve plexuses

### 31 pairs of spinal nerves

| Region | Pairs |
|---|---|
| Cervical | 8 (C1 to C8) |
| Thoracic | 12 (T1 to T12) |
| Lumbar | 5 (L1 to L5) |
| Sacral | 5 (S1 to S5) |
| Coccygeal | 1 |
| **Total** | **31** |

C1 exits between the occipital bone and C1 vertebra. C8 exits between C7 and T1 vertebrae. This is why there are 8 cervical nerves but only 7 cervical vertebrae.

## Nerve Plexuses

| Plexus | Spinal nerves | Major regions and nerves |
|---|---|---|
| Cervical | C1 to C4 | Posterior neck and head. **Phrenic nerve** (innervates diaphragm) |
| Brachial | C5 to T1 | Upper limb. **Median nerve** and other major arm nerves |
| Lumbar | Lumbar nerves | Pelvic region, anterior thigh. **Femoral nerve** |
| Sacral | L4 to L5, S1 to S4 | Hip and lower limb. **Sciatic nerve** (largest nerve in the body) |

**T2 to T11 do not form plexuses.** They continue as **intercostal nerves** between the ribs.

### Clinically important nerves

- **Phrenic nerve:** cervical plexus to diaphragm. C3, C4, C5 keep the diaphragm alive
- **Median nerve:** brachial plexus to anterior forearm and palm
- **Femoral nerve:** lumbar plexus to anterior thigh
- **Sciatic nerve:** sacral plexus to posterior thigh and leg. Compression causes **sciatica**

## Cranial Nerves

Twelve pairs attach directly to the brain, designated I to XII. May be sensory, motor or mixed. Serve head and neck functions. Some provide parasympathetic innervation to thoracic and abdominal organs.

### The vagus nerve (CN X)

Major parasympathetic nerve. Mixed (sensory and motor). Longest cranial nerve.

- **Origin:** medulla oblongata
- **Course:** neck, thorax, abdomen
- **Sensory:** throat, thoracic organs, abdominal organs, part of the external ear
- **Motor:** pharynx and larynx muscles (swallowing, speech)
- **Parasympathetic:** regulates heart rate, promotes digestion, influences smooth muscle and glands in thoracic and abdominal organs

## Reflexes and Reflex Arcs

A reflex is a rapid, automatic, usually involuntary response to a stimulus.

**Functions:** protection, homeostasis, coordination of posture and movement.

### Components of a reflex arc

| Component | Function |
|---|---|
| Receptor | Detects the stimulus (pain, temperature, pressure) |
| Sensory neuron | Carries the impulse from receptor to spinal cord |
| Integration centre | Usually in the spinal cord. Processes information, determines the response |
| Motor neuron | Carries the command from spinal cord to effector |
| Effector | The muscle or gland that produces the response |

### Withdrawal reflex sequence

1. Hot surface activates pain receptors in the skin
2. Sensory neurons carry signals to the spinal cord
3. Interneurons process information in the spinal cord
4. Motor neurons activated
5. Arm muscles contract, pulling the hand away

---

## Somatic Motor Pathway

A single motor neuron runs from the CNS to skeletal muscle.

- **Upper motor neurons:** in the CNS. Plan and initiate movement
- **Lower motor neurons:** in the spinal cord or cranial nerve nuclei. Axons exit via the ventral root or cranial nerve and directly activate skeletal muscle

## Sensory Perception

- **Sensation:** activation of sensory receptor cells by a stimulus
- **Perception:** CNS processing of sensory information into a meaningful pattern
- **Transduction:** conversion of stimuli into electrochemical signals

Not every sensation reaches conscious awareness.

### General vs special senses

| Category | Characteristics | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| General senses | Distributed throughout the body | Touch, pain, temperature, proprioception, visceral sensations |
| Special senses | Specialised sensory organs | Vision, hearing, equilibrium, taste, smell |

### Vision summary

- Three eye layers: fibrous tunic (sclera and cornea), vascular tunic (choroid, ciliary body, iris), neural tunic (retina)
- Six extraocular muscles controlled by CN III, IV, VI
- Anterior cavity holds aqueous humour. Posterior cavity holds vitreous humour
- Optic disc is the blind spot (no photoreceptors)
- Pupil constriction and dilation is autonomic (parasympathetic constricts)

### Hearing and equilibrium summary

- External ear: auricle, external auditory canal, tympanic membrane
- Middle ear: malleus, incus, stapes. Eustachian tube equalises pressure
- Inner ear: **cochlea** (hearing) and **vestibule** (equilibrium)
- **Utricle:** horizontal linear acceleration. **Saccule:** vertical linear acceleration. **Semicircular canals:** rotational movement
- Vestibular signals travel to brainstem and cerebellum for balance, posture and coordination
