## Three Types of Muscle Tissue

| Type | Features | Control and location |
|---|---|---|
| Skeletal | Long cylindrical fibres, multinucleate, striated (myofibrils in striped pattern) | Attached mainly to bones. **Voluntary** |
| Cardiac | Striated fibres with one central nucleus, specialised **intercalated discs** | Heart only (myocardium). **Involuntary** |
| Smooth | Smaller cells with one central nucleus, arranged in sheets or bundles, not striated | Walls of hollow organs, blood vessels. **Involuntary** |

**Intercalated discs** allow nerve impulses to travel between cardiac muscle cells, so the heart contracts as one coordinated unit.

**Smooth muscle types:** visceral (single-unit) and multiunit. Characteristics: autorhythmic (contracts without external stimulation), responds to stretching, maintains constant tension, consistent contraction strength.

## Four Properties of Muscle Tissue

| Property | Definition |
|---|---|
| Contractility | Ability to shorten forcefully and produce movement |
| Excitability | Ability to respond to stimulation, particularly from nerves |
| Extensibility | Ability to stretch |
| Elasticity | Ability to recoil and return to normal shape after stretching or contraction |

## Microscopic Anatomy of Skeletal Muscle

- **Skeletal muscle cells:** multinucleate, surrounded by plasma membrane called the **sarcolemma**. Oval nuclei lie just beneath it
- **Myofibrils:** long ribbon-like organelles filling most of the cytoplasm. Their precise alignment produces the striated appearance
- **Sarcomeres:** myofibrils consist of sarcomeres arranged end to end. Sarcomeres are the **basic contractile units**
- **Myofilaments:** each sarcomere contains thick and thin protein filaments
- **Thick filaments:** composed mainly of **myosin**. Contain ATPase enzymes that split ATP for contraction energy. Myosin projections called **cross bridges** attach to thin filaments during contraction
- **Thin filaments:** contain **actin** and regulatory proteins. Anchored to the **Z discs** of the sarcomere
- **Sarcoplasmic reticulum:** specialised smooth ER surrounding each myofibril. Stores **calcium** and releases it when contraction is required

## Nerve Stimulation and Action Potential: Six Steps

1. Nerve impulse reaches axon terminals of a motor neuron
2. Terminals release **acetylcholine (ACh)**
3. ACh increases permeability of the sarcolemma. Na+ moves in, K+ diffuses out
4. Additional channels open, Na+ enters, producing an **action potential**
5. Action potential travels across the entire sarcolemma and initiates contraction
6. Enzymes break down ACh into acetic acid and choline, so one impulse equals one contraction, then relaxation until the next stimulus

**Neuromuscular junction:** the point where nerve cell meets muscle cell.

---

## Motor Units

- A motor unit is one motor neuron plus all the muscle fibres it supplies
- All fibres in one motor unit contract together when stimulated
- One motor nerve cell commonly supplies around 150 muscle fibres (range roughly 10 to many more)

---

## Muscle Tone

- Involuntary contraction of a small number of motor units
- Keeps skeletal muscles firm even when relaxed
- Does not normally produce movement
- Important for maintaining posture
- Muscle tone in blood vessel walls helps maintain blood pressure

## Arrangement of Muscle Fascicles

| Arrangement | Description |
|---|---|
| Circular | Concentric circles around body openings. Close when they contract (sphincters) |
| Convergent | Converge toward a single insertion. Produces a triangular muscle |
| Parallel | Fascicles run parallel to the long axis. Fusiform is a modification with an expanded central belly |
| Pennate | Short fascicles attach obliquely to a central tendon. Unipennate (one side), bipennate (two sides), multipennate (several sides) |

## Naming Skeletal Muscles

- **Fibre direction:** rectus (straight or parallel), oblique (slanting)
- **Relative size:** maximus, minimus, longus
- **Location:** the bone or region they overlie (temporalis, frontalis)
- **Number of origins:** biceps (2), triceps (3), quadriceps (4)
- **Attachments:** origin and insertion in the name
- **Shape:** distinctive shape reflected in the name
- **Action:** flexor, extensor, adductor

---

## Interactions Among Skeletal Muscles

| Role | Function |
|---|---|
| Prime mover (agonist) | Main muscle producing a movement |
| Antagonist | Opposes or reverses the prime mover. Stretched and relaxed while prime mover contracts |
| Synergist | Assists prime mover. Produces the same movement or reduces unwanted movement |
| Fixator | Specialised synergist. Stabilises the origin of the prime mover or holds a bone still |

---

## Five Golden Rules of Skeletal Muscles

1. Every skeletal muscle crosses at least one joint (few exceptions)
2. The bulk of a muscle usually lies **proximal** to the joint it crosses
3. Every skeletal muscle has at least two attachments: **origin** and **insertion**
4. Skeletal muscles can **pull** but cannot **push**
5. During contraction, the **insertion moves toward the origin**

## Major Regional Muscles: Trunk

- **Pectoralis major:** upper anterior thorax. Adducts and flexes arm
- **External intercostals:** raise rib cage during breathing
- **Internal intercostals:** depress rib cage during forceful exhalation
- **Diaphragm:** dome-shaped. Contracts and flattens during inspiration
- **Scalene muscles:** elevate first two ribs during inspiration
- **Rectus abdominis:** superficial, strap-like. Anterior abdominal wall
- **External and internal obliques:** lateral abdominal wall
- **Transversus abdominis:** deepest abdominal muscle, horizontal fibres
- **Trapezius:** extends neck. Elevates, depresses, adducts and stabilises scapula
- **Latissimus dorsi:** lower back. Powerful arm downstroke
- **Erector spinae:** extends back, controls bending at the waist
- **Quadratus lumborum:** flexes spine separately, extends lumbar spine together

---

## Major Regional Muscles: Upper Limb

- **Deltoid:** rounded shoulder. Prime mover of arm abduction
- **Biceps brachii:** prime mover of forearm flexion, plus supinator
- **Brachialis:** deep to biceps. Important elbow flexor
- **Brachioradialis:** weak. Assists forearm movement
- **Triceps brachii:** prime mover of elbow extension. Antagonist of biceps

---

## Major Regional Muscles: Lower Limb

- **Gluteus maximus:** powerful hip extensor
- **Gluteus medius:** hip abductor. Steadies pelvis during walking
- **Iliopsoas** (iliacus + psoas): prime mover of hip flexion
- **Adductor group:** adducts thighs
- **Hamstring group** (biceps femoris, semimembranosus, semitendinosus): posterior thigh, act at knee
- **Sartorius:** most superficial thigh muscle. Synergist for seated or crossed-leg position
- **Quadriceps group** (rectus femoris plus three vastus muscles): powerful knee extension
- **Tibialis anterior:** anterior leg to tarsal bones
- **Extensor digitorum longus:** extends toes, dorsiflexes foot
- **Fibularis muscles:** plantar flex and evert foot
- **Gastrocnemius:** prime mover of plantar flexion
- **Soleus:** deep to gastrocnemius. Plantar flexion. Does not affect the knee

## Common Muscular Disorders

| Disorder | Key points |
|---|---|
| Muscular dystrophy | Genetic. Absence or deficiency of **dystrophin**. Progressive, irreversible muscle breakdown. Around 30 types. May begin infancy to adulthood |
| Sprains | Sudden twisting injury of a joint. May involve hyperextension, capsule or ligament tearing. Bleeding into the joint possible. Whiplash is a spinal sprain |
| Strains | Microscopic tears or overstretching of muscles or tendons. From forceful movement, heavy lifting, sudden movement |
| Tendonitis | Tendon inflammation from overuse. Repeated use can tear the tendon. Common sites: shoulder, knee, wrist |
| Muscle cramps and spasms | Involuntary forceful contraction. Causes include dehydration, medications, unknown. May act protectively |
| Fibromyalgia | Pain in muscles, bones and joints. Soft-tissue tenderness at multiple sites. Idiopathic. Aggravated by cold or humid weather, fatigue, strenuous exercise |

## Aging and Muscle Tissue

- Loss of muscle fibres begins around **age 25**
- Substantial muscle mass loss by around age 80
- **Weight-bearing exercise** can slow the loss
- Action potentials may slow as axons decrease
- Muscle fibres may shift toward slower fibre types with aging or disuse
