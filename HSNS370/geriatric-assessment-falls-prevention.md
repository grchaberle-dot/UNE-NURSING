# Geriatric Assessment & Falls Prevention

**Unit:** HSNS370  
**Module ID:** `geriatricAssessment`

> Apply comprehensive geriatric assessment, SOAP for unwitnessed falls, falls prevention strategies and dementia-sensitive pain tools in aged care.

---

## Learn

### Comprehensive geriatric assessment

- **Purpose:** Comprehensive geriatric assessment (CGA) identifies strengths, risks, needs and goals so care can be coordinated rather than focused on one symptom.
- **Dimensions:** A multidimensional assessment includes physical, cognitive, psychological, social, cultural, spiritual and functional domains, as well as family context.
- **Outcome:** Findings should lead to an individualised plan, review of priorities and coordinated multidisciplinary follow-up.

### Assessment tools

- **Falls Risk Assessment Tool:** Screens for factors associated with falls and helps guide prevention planning.
- **Katz ADL:** Assesses independence in basic activities of daily living such as bathing, dressing, toileting, transferring, continence and feeding.
- **Psychogeriatric Assessment Scales:** Support assessment of cognitive and depressive symptoms in older people; results require clinical interpretation.

### Pain and dementia

- **PAINAD:** The Pain Assessment in Advanced Dementia tool uses observable behaviours such as breathing, vocalisation, facial expression, body language and consolability.
- **Abbey Pain Scale:** The Abbey Pain Scale supports observation of pain behaviours in people who cannot reliably self-report.
- **Principle:** Use self-report whenever possible, then combine validated observation with history, examination, response to treatment and reassessment.

### SOAP after an unwitnessed fall

- **S - subjective:** Record what was reported or heard: the personal care worker heard a thump, found the resident on the floor, reported possible head strike and pain, and noted a full water jug.
- **O - objective:** Record observed findings: full GCS, hip tenderness, pupils equal and reactive to light, forehead abrasion and medium falls risk.
- **A/P:** Analyse possible causes and risks, then document immediate actions, escalation, monitoring, investigations and prevention follow-up.

### Immediate response to a fall

- **Primary survey:** Check responsiveness, airway, breathing and circulation; do not move the person until injury and safety have been considered.
- **Neurological assessment:** Assess GCS, pupils, behaviour, orientation and new neurological symptoms; repeat observations as indicated.
- **Escalation:** Follow head-injury and local falls protocols, obtain urgent medical review when indicated, assess pain and document clearly.

### Clinical reasoning and differentials

- **Acute causes:** Consider acute illness, infection, dehydration, pain, hypoglycaemia or other metabolic factors.
- **Medication and circulation:** Review medicines, polypharmacy, hypotension, syncope and orthostatic changes.
- **Function and environment:** Consider peripheral neuropathy, musculoskeletal problems, footwear, vision, hazards and the person's usual gait.

### Falls prevention assessment

- **Mobility:** Assess gait, balance, transfers, strength, footwear, vision and use of mobility aids.
- **Clinical review:** Review medicines, orthostatic hypotension, continence, hydration, pain and cognition.
- **Environment:** Check lighting, flooring, clutter, call bell access and safe routes to the toilet and essential items.

### An individualised prevention plan

- **Activity:** Encourage safe mobility and refer to physiotherapy or other services for strength, balance and gait support.
- **Foot and vision care:** Address footwear, podiatry and visual problems where relevant.
- **Daily needs:** Support hydration, regular toileting, appropriate call-bell use and a plan that reflects the person's goals and abilities.

### Chronic follow-up: diabetes and cognition

- **Diabetes:** Monitor glucose-related risks, neuropathy, vision and medication effects that may increase falls or functional decline.
- **Cognition:** Track changes from baseline and investigate acute delirium rather than attributing a sudden change to dementia.
- **Support:** Coordinate family, medical, nursing and allied health input and review whether the plan remains effective.

### Chronic follow-up: skin and sleep

- **Skin:** Assess skin conditions, pressure risk, pain and effects on mobility or sleep.
- **Sleep:** Explore sleep pattern, night-time toileting, sedating medicines, environment and possible underlying illness.
- **Safety:** Avoid treating a fall risk by reflexively restricting mobility; balance prevention with dignity, function and participation.

### Chronic follow-up: hypertension and medicines

- **Blood pressure:** Review symptoms, lying and standing blood pressure where indicated, hydration and medicines that may contribute to dizziness or falls.
- **Polypharmacy:** Reconcile all prescribed, over-the-counter and complementary medicines and seek prescriber or pharmacist review.
- **Shared plan:** Explain changes, monitor response and involve the older person in decisions.

### Chronic follow-up: constipation and hydration

- **Constipation:** Assess bowel pattern, discomfort, mobility, diet, fluid intake and medicines; constipation can contribute to distress and unsafe toileting.
- **Hydration:** Offer fluids regularly while considering swallowing ability, preferences and any fluid restriction.
- **Review:** Use trends and patient or family observations to evaluate whether interventions improve comfort and safety.

## Practice (7 questions)

**Q1.** What is the main purpose of comprehensive geriatric assessment?

- To assess only mobility
- **To identify multidimensional needs and coordinate an individualised plan** (correct)
- To replace all clinical judgement with a score
- To determine whether a person should be restrained

**Q2.** Match each tool with its principal use.

- Katz ADL -> Basic activities of daily living and functional independence
- PAINAD -> Observable pain indicators in advanced dementia
- Falls Risk Assessment Tool -> Screening factors associated with falls
- Abbey Pain Scale -> Observed pain behaviours when self-report is unreliable

**Q3.** Which finding belongs in the objective part of the fall SOAP note?

- The personal care worker heard a thump
- The resident reports hip pain
- **The resident has a full GCS and a forehead abrasion** (correct)
- The resident says the water jug was full

**Q4.** After an unwitnessed fall with a possible head strike, the nurse should first complete a primary survey and follow the head-injury protocol.

- Answer: **True** - Immediate safety, primary survey, neurological assessment and appropriate escalation take priority before routine prevention planning.

**Q5.** Put the immediate response to an unwitnessed fall in the most appropriate order.

1. Ensure scene and patient safety and perform a primary survey
2. Assess neurological status, pain and possible injuries
3. Escalate according to head-injury and falls protocols
4. Document, monitor and develop follow-up prevention actions

**Q6.** Which is an appropriate falls prevention intervention?

- Keep every resident in bed
- Remove mobility aids to prevent walking
- **Review medicines and orthostatic hypotension while encouraging safe mobility** (correct)
- Restrict fluids for all residents

**Q7.** A sudden change in cognition in a person with dementia should automatically be documented as normal dementia progression.

- Answer: **False** - An acute change may indicate delirium or illness and requires assessment for reversible causes and timely escalation.
