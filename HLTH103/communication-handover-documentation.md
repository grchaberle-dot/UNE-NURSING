# Communication, Handover & Documentation

**Unit:** HLTH103  
**Module ID:** `commHandoverExtended`

> Connect therapeutic communication, ISBAR, cultural safety in communication, legal documentation and national standards for clinical handover.

---

## Learn

### Why communication matters

- **Relationships:** Clear, respectful communication builds therapeutic relationships and helps a person participate in decisions about care.
- **Safety:** Communication supports teamwork, identifies changes in condition and reduces omissions at transitions. Poor communication can directly compromise patient safety.

### Active listening

- **Presence:** Give attention, minimise distractions, use appropriate non-verbal attending and allow enough time for the person to speak.
- **Check:** Summarise what you heard and invite correction. Listening is more than waiting for a turn to talk.

### Open questions and reflection

- **Explore:** Open questions such as 'What concerns you most today?' encourage the person to describe their experience rather than supplying a yes-or-no answer.
- **Reflect:** Reflection of feeling or meaning demonstrates attention and can help a person clarify what they need.

### Paraphrasing and empathy

- **Paraphrase:** Restate the essential message in your own words to check shared meaning without changing the person's account.
- **Empathy:** Acknowledge the person's feelings and perspective without claiming to have the same experience or making promises you cannot keep.

### Respect and plain language

- **Respect:** Use a non-judgemental tone, protect dignity and ask permission before sensitive topics or procedures.
- **Jargon:** Avoid unexplained clinical terms. Use plain language, then check understanding rather than assuming nodding means comprehension.

### Checking understanding

- **Teach-back:** Ask the person to explain the plan in their own words. This checks how well the explanation worked; it is not a test of the patient.
- **Clarify:** Correct misunderstandings promptly, invite questions and adapt communication for language, hearing, cognition, health literacy and distress.

### Cultural sensitivity and safety

- **Responsibility:** Culturally safe communication is a core nursing responsibility when caring for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples and multicultural communities.
- **Humility:** Ask about communication preferences rather than relying on stereotypes. Respect different meanings of silence, eye contact, family roles and decision-making.

### ISBAR: a structured handover

- **I-S:** Identify yourself, your role, the patient and the location. State the immediate Situation and why you are calling or handing over.
- **B-A-R:** Give relevant Background, your current Assessment and a clear Recommendation: what is needed, by whom and when.

### ISBAR in practice

- **Acute care:** In an acute setting, lead with urgent change, observations, risk and escalation needs. Do not bury a time-critical concern in a long history.
- **Aged care:** In aged care, include baseline function, cognition, preferences, advance care planning and relevant family or substitute decision-maker information.

### Effective clinical handover

- **Quality:** Handover should be accurate, clear, timely, structured and relevant to the receiving clinician's decisions.
- **Continuity:** Include outstanding tasks, risks, changes, escalation plans and opportunities for questions. Handover is a transfer of responsibility, not merely a data dump.

### National standards

- **NSQHS alignment:** Structured clinical handover supports the National Safety and Quality Health Service standards by making essential information available at points of care transition.
- **Local practice:** Follow organisational policy and scope of practice. Use the approved handover tool, confirm the receiver and escalate concerns through the required pathway.

### Legal documentation

- **Record:** Clinical records are legal documents. Document assessment, care, response, communication, escalation and decisions accurately and contemporaneously.
- **Accountability:** A clear record supports continuity, professional accountability, clinical reasoning, review and the person's legal rights.

### Documentation best practice

- **Objective:** Use factual, relevant, respectful language. Record the person's words when important, distinguish observation from interpretation and avoid judgemental labels.
- **Integrity:** Date and time entries, identify the author, correct errors according to policy and never erase, backdate or falsify a record.

### Non-verbal communication

- **Presence:** An open posture, calm voice, appropriate distance and attentive facial expression can support trust and reduce anxiety.
- **Cultural variation:** Eye contact, silence, touch, gestures and personal space have culturally variable meanings. Observe, ask respectfully and avoid interpreting one cue in isolation.

## Practice (8 questions)

**Q1.** In ISBAR, what does the final R represent?

- Review
- **Recommendation** (correct)
- Record
- Reaction

**Q2.** Place the ISBAR elements in order.

1. Identify
2. Situation
3. Background
4. Assessment
5. Recommendation

**Q3.** Which question best checks a patient's understanding of discharge instructions?

- You understand that, don't you?
- **Can you explain in your own words how you will follow the plan at home?** (correct)
- Did I speak loudly enough?
- Would you like me to finish quickly?

**Q4.** A clinical record should include objective, relevant information and be completed according to documentation policy.

- Answer: **True** - Accurate, timely and objective documentation supports communication, continuity, accountability and the person's legal rights.

**Q5.** Match each therapeutic communication strategy with its purpose.

- Open question -> Encourages a fuller account
- Paraphrasing -> Checks shared meaning
- Empathy -> Acknowledges the person's feelings and perspective
- Teach-back -> Checks how well an explanation was understood

**Q6.** Which handover is most effective?

- A long unstructured account with no recommendation
- **A clear, timely, accurate and structured account including risks and outstanding actions** (correct)
- A handover based on memory after the shift ends
- A handover using unexplained abbreviations and jargon

**Q7.** The meaning of eye contact and silence is identical across all cultures.

- Answer: **False** - Non-verbal behaviours have culturally variable meanings. Nurses should observe without stereotyping and ask about communication preferences when appropriate.

**Q8.** When correcting an error in a clinical record, the nurse should:

- Erase the original entry completely
- Backdate a replacement entry
- **Follow organisational correction policy while preserving record integrity** (correct)
- Ask another person to sign over it
