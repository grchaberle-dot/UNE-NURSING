# Organisational Change & Leadership

**Unit:** HSNS374  
**Module ID:** `orgChange`

> Apply Lewin's change model, transformational leadership, power sources and influence tactics to drive safe, effective change in healthcare.

---

## Learn

### Culture and the need for change

- **Culture:** Organisational culture includes shared values, assumptions, routines and ways of working. It can support safety, or make unsafe practices seem normal.
- **Forces:** Demographic change, multicultural communities, technology and economic pressures can create a need to adapt healthcare services.

### Planned and imposed change

- **Planned:** Planned change is intentionally designed, communicated and implemented with time for consultation, preparation, learning and evaluation.
- **Imposed:** Imposed change is experienced as decided elsewhere or introduced with limited choice. It may be necessary, but poor explanation and involvement can increase resistance.

### Why employees resist

- **Threats:** Resistance may reflect perceived loss of status, privacy, control, competence, relationships or job security, not simply unwillingness to cooperate.
- **Trust:** Inadequate consultation, previous unfulfilled promises and low trust can make a proposed change feel unsafe or unfair.

### Lewin: unfreezing

- **Purpose:** Unfreezing challenges the status quo and builds readiness. Leaders explain why change is needed, listen to concerns and make the case for action.
- **Preparation:** Use evidence, stakeholder mapping, honest communication and psychological support to create a foundation for movement.

### Lewin: movement

- **Action:** Movement is the implementation phase: introduce the new practice, pilot it where appropriate, provide education and support people as they learn.
- **Adapt:** Use feedback and early results to identify barriers, adjust the approach and keep the change connected to patient and staff needs.

### Lewin: refreezing

- **Embed:** Refreezing evaluates the change, reinforces effective practice and embeds it in policy, orientation, supervision, measures and everyday routines.
- **Sustain:** Without reinforcement, teams may return to previous habits. Monitor outcomes and continue to address new risks or unintended effects.

### Transformational leadership

- **Vision:** Transformational leaders create and communicate a shared vision, model the desired change and connect the work with meaningful patient and professional values.
- **Thinking:** They encourage creative thinking, challenge assumptions and support people to contribute solutions rather than merely comply.

### Relational leadership

- **Relationships:** Relational leadership builds trust through listening, respect, collaboration and attention to individual development.
- **Practice:** Leaders make space for diverse voices, recognise expertise and provide coaching, feedback and support during uncertainty.

### Sources of power

- **Legitimate power:** Power comes from a formal position, role or authority to make decisions.
- **Expert power:** Power comes from knowledge, skills, experience or credibility. Coercive power relies on threat or penalty; dependency power arises when others rely on scarce resources or expertise.

### Influence tactics I

- **Evidence and values:** Legitimacy refers to rules or authority; rational persuasion uses evidence and reasoning; inspirational appeals connect change with values, purpose and hope.
- **Involvement:** Consultation invites people to shape the plan, increasing ownership and surfacing practical risks.

### Influence tactics II

- **Exchange and personal appeals:** Exchange offers valued resources or support in return for cooperation; personal appeals draw on loyalty or relationships.
- **Social tactics:** Ingratiation uses praise or friendliness; pressure uses demands or threats; coalition builds support from others. Use power ethically and avoid manipulation.

### The most useful combination

- **Effective change:** For healthcare change, rational persuasion, consultation and inspirational appeals are often especially effective because they combine evidence, participation and shared purpose.
- **Balance:** No single tactic works in every context. Match influence to readiness, risk and relationships while protecting fairness, autonomy and psychological safety.

### Ten-step change plan

- **Steps 1-5:** Diagnose culture; explain the reason; consult staff and consumers; involve formal and informal leaders; and use relevant expertise.
- **Steps 6-10:** Pilot the change; address fairness; build relationships; monitor outcomes and experience; then reinforce and embed what works.

### Change for safe care

- **Measure:** A change plan should monitor patient outcomes, staff experience, equity, unintended consequences and whether the new practice is actually sustained.
- **Leadership:** Safe leaders remain transparent about uncertainty, respond to feedback and adjust rather than defending a failing plan.

## Practice (9 questions)

**Q1.** Place Lewin's stages in order.

1. Unfreezing
2. Movement
3. Refreezing

**Q2.** Which activity belongs mainly to Lewin's unfreezing stage?

- Embed the new process in policy after evaluation
- **Challenge the status quo and build readiness** (correct)
- Stop collecting feedback
- Return to the former practice

**Q3.** Which combination is identified as especially effective for healthcare change?

- Pressure, coercion and threats
- **Rational persuasion, consultation and inspirational appeals** (correct)
- Ingratiation, secrecy and personal appeals only
- Avoidance, delay and coalition against staff

**Q4.** Match each power source with its basis.

- Legitimate power -> Formal role or authority
- Expert power -> Knowledge, skills or experience
- Coercive power -> Threat of penalty or punishment
- Dependency power -> Control of a resource or expertise others need

**Q5.** Employee resistance always means a person is unwilling to support safe care.

- Answer: **False** - Resistance can signal fear, loss of status or privacy, inadequate consultation, low trust or unfulfilled promises. Listening may reveal risks that need to be addressed.

**Q6.** What is a transformational leader most likely to do?

- Keep the purpose secret
- **Model the change, share a vision and support creative thinking** (correct)
- Rely only on formal authority
- Discourage individual development

**Q7.** Place the first five steps of the change-management plan in order.

1. Diagnose culture
2. Explain the reason
3. Consult
4. Involve leaders
5. Use expertise

**Q8.** Refreezing means evaluating, reinforcing and embedding an effective change in routine practice.

- Answer: **True** - Refreezing stabilises the new practice through evaluation, reinforcement, policy, education, supervision and monitoring.

**Q9.** A manager invites nurses to shape a medication-safety pilot and uses their feedback to revise it. Which influence tactic is this?

- **Consultation** (correct)
- Pressure
- Coercion
- Ingratiation
