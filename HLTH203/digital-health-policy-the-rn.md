# Digital Health Policy & the RN

**Unit:** HLTH203  
**Module ID:** `digitalHealthPolicy`

> Navigate Australia's National Digital Health Strategy, Privacy Act obligations, and the NMBA's digital ethics expectations for registered nurses.

---

## Learn

### What is digital health?

- **WHO:** Digital health is the field of knowledge and practice associated with the development and use of digital technologies to improve health.
- **Australia:** The Australian Digital Health Agency describes digital health as the use of digital technologies to improve the delivery of health care and help people manage their health.
- **Nursing relevance:** Digital health includes electronic records, decision-support tools, telehealth, apps and connected devices used across the care journey.

### Australia's national direction

- **Strategy:** The National Digital Health Strategy sets a coordinated direction for safer, more connected and person-centred digital health in Australia.
- **Blueprint:** The Digital Health Blueprint 2023-2033 provides a longer-term plan for a modern, integrated digital health system.
- **RN contribution:** Registered nurses help turn policy into safe practice by using systems accurately, supporting patients and identifying access barriers.

### The political perspective

- **Government role:** Governments shape digital health through legislation, standards, funding, infrastructure and national programs.
- **Investment:** Funding decisions influence whether regional services, communities and health workforces can obtain reliable digital systems.
- **Global commitment:** WHO Health Assembly resolution WHA58.28 recognised the potential of eHealth to strengthen health systems and improve outcomes.

### Socio-cultural context

- **Health literacy:** People need to find, understand, evaluate and use digital health information and services; literacy is shaped by language, culture and experience.
- **Equity:** A digital service can widen inequity when people lack devices, connectivity, affordability, accessibility or confidence.
- **Framework:** The Digital Health Literacy Framework supports consideration of skills, access, trust, communication and the context in which people use technology.

### Digital inclusion and disparity

- **Identify:** Ask about access, data costs, device availability, disability, language, privacy at home and confidence rather than assuming digital access.
- **Respond:** Offer a non-digital alternative, interpreter or support person where appropriate, and allow extra time for teaching.
- **Partnership:** Co-design and culturally safe communication help ensure digital health benefits are shared across socio-economic groups.

### Privacy Act 1988 and APPs

- **Privacy law:** The Privacy Act 1988 regulates handling of personal information by covered organisations, including health information.
- **APPs:** Australian Privacy Principles address collection, use and disclosure, data quality and security, access and correction, and transparency.
- **Practice:** Collect only what is needed, use information for an authorised purpose, secure it and give patients appropriate access and correction pathways.

### State and territory obligations

- **Health Records Act 2001:** In Victoria, the Health Records Act 2001 sets health privacy requirements, including how health information is collected, used, disclosed and accessed.
- **Jurisdiction:** RNs must follow the privacy and records legislation that applies in their state or territory as well as organisational policy.
- **Record quality:** Clear, contemporaneous, factual documentation supports continuity of care and protects patient privacy.

### My Health Records Act 2012

- **Purpose:** The My Health Records Act 2012 governs the national electronic health record system and its information-handling framework.
- **Consent and control:** Patients have rights to control access, manage privacy settings and know how their information is used, subject to the system's legal rules.
- **RN duty:** Access a record only when authorised and needed for care, protect credentials and explain access or privacy options within scope.

### Confidentiality and informed consent

- **Confidentiality:** Keep patient information private in conversations, screens, messages, photographs, handover and social media.
- **Consent:** Explain what digital information will be collected, shared or recorded, check understanding and respect a refusal where care can safely proceed.
- **Minimum necessary:** Share the minimum relevant information with the right people for the right clinical purpose.

### Professional boundaries online

- **Boundaries:** Do not use personal accounts to contact patients, accept inappropriate social-media connections or discuss identifiable care online.
- **Security:** Use approved systems, strong authentication and private workspaces; do not leave records visible or share passwords.
- **Escalation:** Report a suspected breach promptly through organisational processes and document relevant facts without speculation.

### NMBA digital ethics

- **Code of Ethics:** The NMBA Code of Ethics supports respect, dignity, equity, partnership and advocacy when care is delivered or mediated digitally.
- **Code of Conduct:** The Code of Conduct expects professional, safe and accountable behaviour, including responsible use of information and communication technologies.
- **Accountability:** Digital tools do not transfer professional responsibility: an RN remains accountable for decisions, documentation and escalation.

### Safe digital decision-making

- **Check:** Verify patient identity, data accuracy, permissions, recipient and the clinical context before entering or sharing information.
- **Balance:** Consider benefit, harm, autonomy, privacy, equity and cultural safety rather than treating technology as automatically neutral.
- **Escalate:** Seek guidance when a system is unsafe, a privacy concern arises, consent is unclear or a digital recommendation conflicts with assessment.

## Practice (7 questions)

**Q1.** Which statement best reflects the WHO concept of digital health?

- Only electronic medical records used in hospitals
- **The use of digital technologies and knowledge to improve health and care** (correct)
- Any health information posted on social media
- A replacement for face-to-face nursing assessment

**Q2.** Which action best supports digital health equity?

- Assume every patient has a current smartphone
- Require online communication for all follow-up
- **Ask about access and offer suitable non-digital or supported alternatives** (correct)
- Use technical language to save teaching time

**Q3.** Match each policy or framework with its focus.

- Privacy Act 1988 -> Australian privacy obligations, including the APPs
- My Health Records Act 2012 -> Legal framework for the national electronic health record system
- Digital Health Literacy Framework -> Capabilities and contextual factors affecting digital health use
- NMBA Code of Conduct -> Professional, safe and accountable nursing behaviour

**Q4.** An RN may access any patient's electronic record because the information belongs to the health service.

- Answer: **False** - Access must be authorised and necessary for the RN's role and the patient's care; curiosity or convenience is not a lawful purpose.

**Q5.** Put these steps for responding to a possible digital privacy incident in a sensible order.

1. Contain further disclosure where possible
2. Notify the appropriate supervisor or privacy pathway
3. Document the relevant facts accurately
4. Participate in review and follow-up actions

**Q6.** Which principle is most consistent with informed consent for digital health?

- Consent is unnecessary if a system is convenient
- **Explain the purpose and sharing, check understanding and respect the patient's choice** (correct)
- Only obtain consent after information has already been shared
- Use a family member's agreement for every adult patient

**Q7.** Professional accountability still applies when an RN uses an automated digital recommendation.

- Answer: **True** - The RN must use professional judgement, check the recommendation against assessment and escalate concerns.
