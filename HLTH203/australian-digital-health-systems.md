# Australian Digital Health Systems

**Unit:** HLTH203  
**Module ID:** `auDigitalSystems`

> Master My Health Record, ePrescriptions and telehealth: how they work, their benefits, challenges, and the RN's role in supporting patients.

---

## Learn

### My Health Record at a glance

- **Definition:** My Health Record is Australia's national digital health record system, allowing authorised information to be available across participating services.
- **Content:** Depending on the service and patient choices, it can contain shared health summaries, hospital and specialist information, pathology or imaging reports, prescriptions and immunisation information.
- **Continuity:** It supplements, rather than automatically replaces, local clinical records and clinical communication.

### Access and the opt-out model

- **Access:** Patients and authorised healthcare providers access information through approved systems, with identity, role and privacy controls.
- **Opt-out:** The national system moved to an opt-out model in January 2019; patients retain privacy controls and can choose to restrict access.
- **RN support:** Explain options in plain language and direct patients to the appropriate service for detailed account or access changes.

### Benefits in practice

- **Emergency access:** Relevant information may help clinicians make safer decisions when a patient cannot provide a complete history.
- **Centralised record:** Information from different services can be available in one place, supporting continuity and reducing repeated history-taking.
- **Patient participation:** Patients can view and contribute to their health information according to system functions and permissions.

### My Health Record challenges

- **Trust:** Security concerns, privacy fears and uncertainty about who can see information may reduce engagement.
- **Capability:** Digital literacy, language, disability and access to devices or connectivity affect use.
- **Interoperability:** Different systems may not exchange information consistently, and records may be incomplete or out of date.

### Different patient groups

- **Children and adolescents:** Access and consent arrangements change with age and capacity; privacy and developing autonomy need careful explanation.
- **Older adults:** Offer supported access, large-print or accessible instructions and time for questions rather than assuming low interest or low ability.
- **All groups:** Use an individualised, culturally sensitive approach and check who the patient wants involved.

### ePrescriptions and eScripts

- **Definition:** An ePrescription is a digital version of a paper prescription that can be sent to an authorised pharmacy using an electronic token or an active prescription list.
- **Issuing:** A prescriber creates and legally signs the prescription in an approved prescribing system; a pharmacy then dispenses it through an approved process.
- **Safety:** The digital workflow can improve legibility and reduce some transcription errors, but it does not remove the need for clinical checking.

### ePrescription benefits

- **Efficiency:** Digital transmission can reduce handling of paper and make repeat supply more convenient.
- **Accuracy:** Legible electronic information and system checks can support medication safety.
- **Access:** eScripts may be useful for people in rural or remote areas when connectivity, pharmacy capacity and patient access are adequate.

### ePrescription limitations

- **Technology:** Poor connectivity, device failure or an unavailable system can interrupt the process.
- **Workforce:** Providers and pharmacy staff require training and a reliable workflow.
- **Patient comfort:** Some people prefer paper, cannot use a device confidently or need an accessible alternative; support should be respectful, not coercive.

### Telehealth defined

- **Meaning:** Telehealth delivers health assessment, advice, education, monitoring or support when clinician and patient are in different locations.
- **Channels:** It may use video, telephone or secure text and messaging, depending on clinical need, privacy and patient preference.
- **Limits:** Telehealth is not suitable for every concern and cannot replace urgent physical assessment when examination or immediate intervention is needed.

### Telehealth benefits and risks

- **Benefits:** Telehealth can improve rural and remote access, reduce travel and costs, and reduce some opportunities for cross-infection.
- **Challenges:** Technical problems, privacy in the home, limited examination, training needs and communication difficulties can affect safety.
- **Preparation:** Confirm identity, location, privacy, consent, technology and an escalation or emergency plan before proceeding.

### The RN as digital health facilitator

- **Competency:** RNs need digital health competency: safe system use, privacy awareness, clinical judgement and the ability to recognise limitations.
- **Teaching:** Help patients navigate devices, portals and appointments using demonstration, teach-back and accessible instructions.
- **Trust:** Be transparent about what technology can and cannot do, protect confidentiality and adapt to the patient's culture, language and preferences.

### Helen's case

- **Context:** Helen is an Aboriginal woman living remotely with chronic disease and difficulty maintaining medication adherence.
- **Potential benefit:** With consent and appropriate support, My Health Record can help authorised services see relevant information and support continuity when local access is limited.
- **Culturally safe care:** The RN should listen to Helen's priorities, involve chosen supports, address connectivity and access barriers, and never assume that a digital solution alone resolves adherence.

## Practice (7 questions)

**Q1.** Which is a potential benefit of My Health Record during an emergency?

- It guarantees that every record is complete and current
- **It may provide authorised clinicians with relevant information when the patient cannot give a full history** (correct)
- It removes the need for assessment
- It automatically gives every family member access

**Q2.** Australia's national My Health Record system moved to an opt-out model in January 2019.

- Answer: **True** - The system changed to an opt-out model in January 2019, while patients retained privacy and access controls.

**Q3.** Match each digital system with its description.

- My Health Record -> National digital record that can share authorised health information
- ePrescription -> Digital version of a prescription issued by an authorised prescriber
- Telehealth -> Care delivered across distance using video, phone or secure messaging
- Teach-back -> Checking understanding by asking the patient to explain or demonstrate

**Q4.** Before starting a telehealth consultation, which check is most important?

- Only whether the patient owns a smartphone
- **Identity, location, privacy, consent and an emergency plan** (correct)
- Whether the patient has used social media
- Whether the consultation can avoid all documentation

**Q5.** Put these telehealth preparation steps in a sensible order.

1. Confirm identity, location and immediate safety
2. Explain the purpose, limitations and obtain consent
3. Check privacy and technology
4. Assess, document and arrange follow-up or escalation

**Q6.** Because an ePrescription is digital, the RN does not need to check medication identity, dose or patient understanding.

- Answer: **False** - Digital prescribing can support safety but does not replace medication reconciliation, clinical checking, education or monitoring.

**Q7.** What is the most culturally safe response when supporting Helen with a digital system?

- Insist she use the system without discussion
- Assume non-adherence is caused by lack of motivation
- **Listen to her priorities, involve chosen supports and address practical access barriers** (correct)
- Speak only to the nearest service provider
