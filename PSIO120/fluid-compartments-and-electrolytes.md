# Fluid Compartments, Electrolytes and Body Chemistry

**Source:** Studocu AU, *The Internal Environment and Body Chemistry*. Australian university context.

---

## The Internal Environment

Consists of cells suspended in fluid, separated by barriers. Maintaining it is homeostasis.

---

## Body Fluid Compartments

| Compartment | Definition |
|---|---|
| Intracellular fluid (ICF) | Fluid inside cells |
| Extracellular fluid (ECF) | Fluid outside cells |
| Interstitial fluid (ISF) | Fluid between cells within tissues |
| Plasma (intravascular) | Fluid within blood vessels |
| Intraocular fluid | Fluid within the eyes |
| Synovial fluid | Fluid within joints |

ICF and ECF contain different solutes and electrolytes, supporting the different functions of cells and their surrounding environment. Interstitial fluid and plasma are both ECF.

---

## Electrolytes and Ions

**Electrolytes** are substances that form ions when dissolved in water. Ions are electrically charged particles found in both ICF and ECF.

- **Cations** are positively charged
- **Anions** are negatively charged

| Ion | Compartment | Key role |
|---|---|---|
| Sodium (Na+) | ECF cation | Fluid balance. Main determinant of ECF volume |
| Potassium (K+) | ICF cation | Fluid balance. Critical for cardiac rhythm |
| Calcium (Ca2+) | Both | Blood clotting. Also muscle contraction and nerve signalling |
| Bicarbonate (HCO3-) | Both | CO2 transport. Main pH buffer |
| Chloride (Cl-) | ECF anion | Digestion |
| Hydrogen (H+) | Both | Respiration and pH balance |

> Sodium lives outside the cell, potassium lives inside. That gradient is what the sodium-potassium pump maintains and what every action potential depends on.

---

## pH Balance

Essential for survival and normal cell function.

- **Acids** release hydrogen ions, which **lowers** pH
- **Bases** use up hydrogen ions, which **raises** pH
- **Neutral pH** is the optimal condition for cell function

> More H+ means more acidic means lower pH. The numbers run counter-intuitively.

## Atoms and Molecules

- **Atoms** are the basic components of all matter
- **Elements** are substances made of the same type of atom
- The human body is primarily **CHON**: carbon, hydrogen, oxygen, nitrogen
- **Molecules** are groups of atoms joined together

---

## Macromolecules

Large organic molecules containing carbon and oxygen. Many are polymers made from smaller monomers.

| Macromolecule | Monomer | Key functions |
|---|---|---|
| Carbohydrates | Monosaccharides | Short-term energy. Helps build cell membranes |
| Lipids | Fatty acids and glycerol | Triglycerides for energy storage, phospholipids for membranes, steroids |
| Proteins | Amino acids | Structural material, biological catalysts, cell communication, movement, defence. **Function depends on shape** |
| Nucleic acids | Nucleotides | Store and interpret genetic information |

### DNA vs RNA

- **DNA** is double-stranded, coiled into a helix. Contains the code used to make proteins
- **RNA** is single-stranded. Interprets DNA and helps create proteins

---

## Chemical Reactions, Enzymes and ATP

- **Chemical reactions** transform substances within the body
- **Enzymes** are proteins that speed up chemical reactions (biological catalysts)
- **ATP** (adenosine triphosphate) is the energy-containing molecule inside cells, supplying energy for cellular activities

> Protein function depends on shape. That is why denaturation destroys function, and why a single wrong amino acid causes sickle cell disease.
