# Non-Australian Source Index

**Purpose:** these sources are held as **reference only**. Per the study rules, non-Australian material is used for structure and mnemonics but **never for values, lab ranges, drug brand names or legal frameworks**, and it does **not** go into the interactive app.

---

## Why this file exists

Lab reference ranges, medication brand names, dosing conventions and legal or regulatory frameworks differ between countries. A US or UK source can be perfectly correct in its own context and still be wrong for an Australian clinical exam or placement.

These sources were uploaded during September 2026 and reviewed. The **concepts** are sound and useful. The **specifics** must always be checked against a UNE unit guide or an Australian clinical guideline before use.

---

## Source hierarchy (the rule)

1. **UNE unit guides** - always authoritative
2. **Australian sources** - OzNurseHub, Australian university pages, Australian clinical guidelines, Healthdirect, state health services
3. **International material** - structure and mnemonics only, never values or law

## Indexed sources

### Harvard nursing study notes (US)

Covers: fire and earthquake safety, nursing process and delegation, medication and nutrition, stress and defence mechanisms, therapeutic communication, respiratory / renal / cardiovascular / neurological disorders, emergency nursing, endocrine and metabolic disorders, electrolytes, acid-base, molecular biology.

**Use for:** conceptual frameworks and structure.
**Do not use for:** lab values, drug names, emergency protocols, delegation law.

### Britannica articles

Covers: skeleton, ossification, cell biology, coagulation cascade, erythropoiesis, haemoglobin detail, blood diseases, coagulation tests, anatomy and human body overviews.

**Use for:** anatomical and physiological description.
**Do not use for:** reference ranges or clinical decision thresholds.

### Pearson General Biology study notes (US)

Covers: metabolism and cellular respiration, redox reactions (OIL RIG mnemonic), proton leak and uncoupling proteins, thermogenesis in brown adipose tissue, reactive oxygen species.

**Use for:** the OIL RIG mnemonic, proton leak concept, ROS concept.
**Do not use for:** ATP yield figures where they conflict with Australian teaching (Australian sources give 30/32 ATP).

### Hiller-Sturmhofel and Bartke, *The Endocrine System: An Overview* (US, 1998)

Covers: hormone classes (steroids, amino acid derivatives, polypeptides), hormone mechanisms of action, negative and positive feedback, short-loop feedback, hypothalamic releasing and inhibiting hormones, anterior and posterior pituitary, adrenal glands, gonads, thyroid, parathyroid, pancreas, HPA / HPG / HPT axes, Addison's disease, Cushing's syndrome.

**Use for:** the three hormone classes, the axis structures, feedback mechanism concepts.
**Do not use for:** any reference values. Note the paper is from 1998 and focuses on alcohol's endocrine effects.

### Global healthy aging material (WHO-derived)

Covers: healthy aging strategies, community and primary care implications, elder mistreatment, sexual health in aging.

**Use for:** global policy context and conceptual frameworks.
**Do not use for:** Australian aged care law, My Aged Care processes, or ACQSC standards. Those must come from Australian sources.

### RCPA Haematology Trainee Handbook 2024 (Australian, but out of scope)

This is Australian (Royal College of Pathologists of Australasia) but it is a **specialist pathologist training program document**: exam structures, portfolio requirements, supervision rules, dissertation criteria.

**Use for:** nothing in the nursing program. Retained only because it was uploaded.
**Note:** the Essential Topics appendices do list haematology laboratory techniques, which could be mildly useful context for understanding what a haematology lab actually does when you order a test.

## Studocu-specific rules

- Studocu material is a **guide and scaffold only**. Flag anything unverified
- Studocu **essays and assessments** are exam-prep reference only. They do not go in the app or the hub as fact, because they could simply be wrong
- Studocu material from **UNE** can be used as a style reference for how UNE expects work presented
- Australian university Studocu notes (Monash, UQ, QUT, JCU, SCU, Federation, Newcastle) have been treated as Australian sources and used in the app
