\uD83D\uDD04 The Core Loop

Every topic runs through the same five stages. Do not skip straight to testing, and do not stop at reading.

  1. Learn: read the content with key terms highlighted
  2. Practice: low-stakes matching, ordering and drag-and-drop to build familiarity
  3. Test: timed quiz, scored, wrong answers flagged
  4. Review: see exactly what you got wrong and why
  5. Retry: re-quiz ONLY the items you failed, until they are all clean
This is spaced repetition plus active recall, which is the strongest evidence-based combination for health sciences. 80% is the pass bar, then revisit the module a week later.

\u26A0\uFE0F What Does Not Work

  • Re-reading notes: feels productive, builds almost nothing. Recognition is not recall.
  • Highlighting: the same problem. You end up with a colourful page you still cannot recall.
  • Copying slides word for word: transcription is not learning. Summarise in your own words instead.
  • Cramming a whole unit the night before: you will pass badly and retain nothing for placement.
  • Studying only what you are good at: it feels great and changes nothing.

\uD83E\uDDE9 Methods That Do Work

Active recall

Close the book and write out everything you remember, then check what you missed. The gap between what you wrote and what is true IS the study list.

Spaced repetition

Revisit a topic at increasing intervals: day 1, day 3, week 1, week 3. The moment it feels slightly hard to recall is exactly when reviewing it is most valuable.

Teach it out loud

Explain the topic to an empty room, a dog, or a partner who did not ask. The moment you stumble is the gap.

Interleaving

Mix related topics in one session rather than blocking one topic for hours. Harder, and it sticks better.

Practice questions first

Attempt questions BEFORE you feel ready. Failing a question then learning the answer beats reading the answer first.

Audio for dead time

Convert detailed notes to audio with Speechify and listen during the commute, the gym or walking the dogs.

\uD83E\uDDE0 Working With an ADHD Brain

  • Short blocks, hard stops: 25 minutes on, 5 off beats a vague three-hour plan you never start
  • One tab, one task: close everything else. Decision fatigue is the enemy
  • Start with the interactive thing: open a quiz, not a textbook. Momentum first, depth second
  • Body doubling: study alongside someone, even on a silent video call
  • Make the next step stupidly obvious: end every session by writing the single next action
  • Visual over verbal: diagrams, colour coding and labelling exercises will always land harder than paragraphs
The interactive app exists for exactly this reason. Clicking and labelling holds attention in a way that reading a page does not.

\uD83D\uDCDA Per-Unit Routine

  1. Before the lecture: skim the unit guide's learning outcomes so you know what you are listening for
  2. During: note only what is NOT already in the slides
  3. Within 24 hours: write a one-page summary in your own words
  4. Same week: build flashcards or a quiz from that summary
  5. Weekly: run through the quizzes for all active topics, not just the newest one
  6. Before assessment: closed-book teach-back, then targeted practice on whatever fell apart

\u2705 Golden Rules

  • UNE unit guides are the authority. Everything else is scaffolding.
  • External notes (Studocu, Etsy, US-based resources) are guides only. Flag anything unverified.
  • Assessment help means scaffolded steps, never ghost-writing.
  • Referencing is APA 7th.
  • "Essay" and "assessment" documents from other students are style examples only, not content sources.
