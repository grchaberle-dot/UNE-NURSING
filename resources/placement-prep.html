800 hours across the degree. Here is how to walk in ready.

Placement requirements are set by UNE and by each health service. Always confirm current requirements with your clinical placement coordinator. This page is a preparation aid, not a compliance checklist.

\u2705 Before Your First Placement

  • Vaccinations: complete all required immunisations as per NSW Health policy
  • Placement eligibility: confirm the university has cleared you
  • Police check and working with children check: as required
  • Hand hygiene and manual handling training: usually completed online before you start
  • Know your scope: as a first-year student you observe and assist under supervision. Know what you may not do.
  • Uniform, shoes, ID: sorted the week before, not the night before
Check with your clinical placement coordinator for the specific requirements for each placement. They differ between health services.

\uD83C\uDF92 What to Actually Take

In your pocket

  • Pen (black), spare pen
  • Small notepad
  • Fob watch with a second hand
  • CHIMNEOPS and ISBAR prompt card
  • Normal vital sign ranges card

In your bag

  • Water bottle and real food
  • Spare socks
  • Hand cream (your hands will suffer)
  • Your learning objectives for the placement
  • Phone charger, kept in the bag not the pocket

\uD83D\uDCDA Frameworks to Know Cold

These four are the difference between looking lost and looking like you have done this before.

  • CHIMNEOPS for written progress notes
  • ISBAR for verbal handover and escalation
  • ANTT for every aseptic procedure
  • ABCDE for any patient who looks unwell
Practise all four in the clinical skills lab before placement so the movements and the words become automatic. Under pressure you fall back on what is automatic.

\uD83D\uDDE3\uFE0F How to Behave on the Floor

  • Ask questions. Clinical facilitators expect it. Silence when you are unsure is dangerous.
  • Say "I have not done this before." Every time. Nobody expects you to have.
  • Tell your supervisor immediately if you contaminate a sterile field or make an error. It is always safer to speak up.
  • Narrate your actions during supervised practice. It lets your supervisor correct you before, not after.
  • Be on time. Punctuality is read as professional commitment.
  • Ask for feedback. Then actually act on it and say what you changed.
  • Take a real break. Skipping breaks does not make you a better student, it makes you a slower one.

\uD83D\uDD10 Privacy on Placement

  • Never photograph anything clinical: no charts, no monitors, no patients, no whiteboards
  • Never discuss patients outside the clinical team, including with family or on social media
  • Discard printed handover sheets into confidential waste at the end of every shift
  • Do not use patient identifiers in your reflective writing
Breaching the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth), your NMBA obligations or a health service's confidentiality policy can result in AHPRA investigation and legal consequences. This ends nursing careers before they start.

\uD83E\uDDE0 Looking After Yourself

  • Discomfort is normal. The first placement feels overwhelming for everyone.
  • Debrief after difficult shifts. Do not carry it home silently.
  • Compassion fatigue and moral distress are normal responses, not weakness.
  • Shift work wrecks sleep. Prioritise 7 to 9 hours, prep meals in advance, keep moving.
  • Nurse and Midwife Support: free 24/7 national service on 1800 667 877
  • Lifeline: 13 11 14. Beyond Blue: 1300 22 4636.
Also build a support network early: classmates, a study group, and a mentor. Most Australian nursing schools run peer mentoring.

\uD83C\uDFDE\uFE0F Armidale Intensive Schools

Mandatory on-campus intensive schools are part of the online BNURS from Trimester 1 onwards. Plan early:

  • Book travel and accommodation as soon as dates are released
  • Arrange leave from work well in advance
  • Arrange dog care for the full period plus travel days
  • Attendance is compulsory: clinical skills are assessed in person
