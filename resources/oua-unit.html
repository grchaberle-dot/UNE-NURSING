\uD83C\uDFAF The Plan

Study one or more Open Universities Australia units before the BNURS starts, then apply for advanced standing so they count toward the 144 credit points.

  • Degree: UNE Bachelor of Nursing (BNURS), Rule A
  • Start: Trimester 1, mid-February 2027, online
  • Structure: 144 credit points, 24 core units
  • Duration: 3 years full time, 6 years part time maximum
  • Also required: mandatory Armidale intensive schools and 800 hours of PEP placement

\u2705 Steps to Take

  1. Confirm with UNE first. Ask Admissions or the Course Coordinator which specific OUA units they will grant advanced standing for toward the BNURS. Get it in writing before enrolling.
  2. Check the credit cap. Universities limit how much advanced standing a degree can accept. Find out the maximum before paying for multiple units.
  3. Check whether it affects the intensive schools or PEP hours. Advanced standing for theory units does not usually reduce placement requirements.
  4. Enrol in the OUA study period that finishes before mid-February 2027 so the result is available when you need it.
  5. Keep every document. Unit outlines, results and assessment descriptions all support a credit application.
  6. Apply for advanced standing formally once you have the result, not verbally.
Do not enrol in an OUA unit and assume it will count. Confirm eligibility with UNE first. An unaccepted unit is money and time you cannot get back.

\uD83D\uDD0D What to Ask UNE

  • Which OUA units are recognised for advanced standing toward the BNURS?
  • What is the maximum advanced standing the BNURS will accept?
  • Does advanced standing change my expected completion date or trimester sequence?
  • Does it change my intensive school or PEP placement obligations?
  • What is the deadline to submit an advanced standing application for a Trimester 1 2027 start?
  • Are there any units I should NOT take because they duplicate BNURS content without matching it?
Send these as one email so you have a single written reply covering everything. Save it.

\uD83D\uDCDA Which Units Are Most Likely to Transfer

Generic health and foundation units usually have the best chance of transferring, because nursing-specific and clinical units tend to be tied to the program's own accreditation and placement structure.

In the BNURS structure, the HLTH-coded units are the more general health and social care units, while HSNS and PSIO units are nursing-specific or physiology-specific. That makes HLTH-type content the more likely candidate for cross-institutional credit.

This is general reasoning about how credit usually works, not advice from UNE. Only UNE can tell you what they will actually accept. Confirm before enrolling.

\uD83D\uDCC5 Timeline to Work Back From

  • Now: email UNE, confirm which units count and the application deadline
  • After confirmation: enrol in an OUA study period that completes with results issued well before mid-February 2027
  • During the unit: keep the unit outline, assessment briefs and final result
  • On completion: submit the formal advanced standing application
  • Mid-February 2027: Trimester 1 begins
Work backwards from the advanced standing deadline, not forwards from today. The deadline is the fixed point.
